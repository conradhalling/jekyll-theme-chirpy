When my wife and I moved to Massachusetts in 2001, I was dismayed to discover
that the local nurseries didn't open until the first week of April because
winter lingered so long. I grew up in Oregon, where the winters are milder, and
you could start buying plants in mid-February. But as the years have gone by and
civilization's waste products warm the world, nurseries in the Boston area are
beginning to open in mid-March.

We had a fairly typical winter here in New England (meaning typical for twenty
years ago — climate change is real), with ice on the Charles River and a fair
amount of snow.

![Snow on February 9](snow_on_2025-02-09.png){:
width="640" height="480" }_Snow on February 9_

Spring has been slow in arriving. This morning the temperature was 29 °F (-2
°C), but the wind made it feel like it was 16 °F (-9 °C), and my hands felt like
they were freezing while I walked the dogs at 7 a.m.

My crocuses, which in the 2020s have started blooming in the last week of
February, bloomed only a few days ago, about three weeks later than our new
normal.

![Crocuses in the front yard](crocuses.png){:
width="480" height="640" }_Crocuses in the front yard_

Late last fall, I bought some pansies and put them in a planter by the east side
of the house. They survived snow, ice, and many nights of very cold weather,
and they're blooming again.

![Pansies in a planter](pansies.png){:
width="640" height="609" }_Pansies in a planter_

I plan to go to [Ricky's Flower Market](https://rickysflowermarket.com) this
week to buy more pansies and a few other plants.
