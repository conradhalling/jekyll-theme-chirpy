When I took guitar lessons in the mid-1970s, I might have owned a fake book
or a copy of *The Real Book*. Or just maybe photocopies of a few songs from
a fake book—my recollection is not very clear.

Today I learned about the history of *The Real Book*, and I suddenly figured out
why *The Real Book* is called *The Real Book*. Sometimes I'm slow at figuring
things like this out.

Anyway, if you want to know more, about seven years ago, Adam Neely posted a
YouTube video, [What is the Real Book? (a jazz
shibboleth)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dD0e5e6wI_A), that neatly
summarizes how *The Real Book* can both be an aid to a musician but also a
hindrance. Four years ago [99% Invisible posted an
article](https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/the-real-book/) about *The Real
Book* that covered many of the same ideas.

The [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/) has many copies of [fake
books](https://archive.org/search?query=music+fake+books).

And if you want a legal copy, go to the [Hal Leonard
website](https://www.halleonard.com/feature/490/real-books). I see digital
copies of Hal Leonard's various Real Books are available from Apple's Books app.
These books include the melodies.

A new alternative is an app such as [iReal Pro](https://www.irealpro.com), but
reviewers say that the drawback of this app is that it provides only the harmony
(chord charts) but not the melody (lead sheets).

I might buy a fake book just to study it, but my ability to play any of the
music on my guitar is about nil. Coming from a family of musicians, I have
music in my blood, but unfortunately I do not have music in my fingers.
