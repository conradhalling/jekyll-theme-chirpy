A few months ago, I posted a list of
[long books that I have read]( {% link _posts/2025-10-06-long-books.md %} ).
Many of those books appear on various lists of great books.

I recently watched a
[YouTube video by Benjamin McEvoy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hDjVHqKR-_k)
about a list of the "fifty greatest books of all time." McEvoy didn't provide
the source of this list, and I was unable to find it with a little web
searching.

By the way,
[Benjamin McEvoy's YouTube videos](https://www.youtube.com/@BenjaminMcEvoy)
about literature are very good, and I recommend them. However, I cringed
when in one video he announced he's a Pisces and that means he prefers books
written by Pisces. That's pseudoscience rubbish.

To return to the point, I reviewed the video about the list of the "fifty
greatest books of all time," and I made this list of the fourteen books I
haven't read yet:

* <cite>Midnight’s Children</cite>, by Salman Rushdie
* <cite>The Magic Mountain</cite>, by Thomas Mann
* <cite>Leaves of Grass</cite>, by Walt Whitman
* <cite>Collected Fiction</cite>, by Jorge Luis Borges
* <cite>The Stories of Anton Chekhov</cite>, by Anton Chekhov
* <cite>Beloved</cite>, by Toni Morrison
* <cite>Gulliver’s Travels</cite>, by Jonathan Swift
* <cite>The Red and the Black</cite>, by Stendahl
* <cite>Absalom! Absalom!</cite>, by William Faulkner
* <cite>One Thousand and One Nights</cite>
* <cite>The Sound and the Fury</cite>, by William Faulkner
* <cite>To the Lighthouse</cite>, by Virginia Woolf
* <cite>The Brothers Karamazov</cite>, by Fyodor Dostoyevsky
* <cite>The Divine Comedy</cite>, by Dante Alighieri

These are eight books from the list that I haven't read _recently_ (in, say, the
past thirty years) and that I would like to read again:

* <cite>Tristram Shandy</cite>, by Laurence Stern
* <cite>David Copperfield</cite>, by Charles Dickens
* <cite>Mrs. Dalloway</cite>, by Virginia Woolf
* <cite>The Trial</cite>, by Franz Kafka
* <cite>Invisible Man</cite>, by Ralph Ellison
* <cite>Catch-22</cite>, by Joseph Heller
* <cite>The Catcher in the Rye</cite>, by J. D. Salinger
* <cite>Crime and Punishment</cite>, by Fyodor Dostoyevsky

So much to read, so little time.
