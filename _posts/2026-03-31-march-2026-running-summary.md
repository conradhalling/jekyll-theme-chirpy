I didn't run the first six days of March because of lingering snow and ice from
the snowstorm of February 22-23, when we received fifteen inches of snow on top
of the fifteen inches of snow that had fallen (and not melted) a couple of weeks
before. In the rest of March, I began making up for lost time, running pretty
steady mileage and increasing the lengths of my runs.

I ran two 5k races this month. The first race was the Rás na hÉireann USA 5k in
Somerville on March 15. I ran to the start before the race, but I walked home
afterwards, stopping at Wicked Café for a decaf latte and a breakfast sandwich.
Based on my surprisingly good finish time, I think the course was short.

The second 5k race was the Spring Classic in Cambridge on March 29. This was the
first in a four-race series (Spring, Summer, Fall, and Winter Classics). I ran
two miles to the start, completed the race, and ran slowly home for a total of a
little more than seven miles for the morning. This race was larger than I
expected, with more than 2,800 finishers. I spent most of this race passing
slower runners and walkers. This is not to say that I am a fast runner; I placed
at position 1,766 out of 2,864 finishers, so 61% of all runners finished ahead
of me. I don't think I could have run much faster than I did.

I am experiencing occasional joint pain in my left knee but no other problems
right now. I'm looking forward to warmer weather and longer distances in April.

I need to change how I do my long runs. I have been stubbornly running without
walking breaks, and last year when my long run reached twelve miles, I got
injured. So I need to overcome my stubbornness and begin taking walking breaks
during my runs. When I first started training for marathons, I used Jeff
Galloway's plan, which used a one-minute walking break every mile. When I got
slower, I just ran five minutes and walked one minute.
