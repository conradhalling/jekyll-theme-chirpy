I am reading Elizabeth Wilson's excellent biography of Jacqueline du Pré,
<cite>[Jacqueline du Pré: Her Life, Her Music, Her Legend](https://www.skyhorsepublishing.com/arcade-publishing/9781611458251/jacqueline-du-pre/)</cite>.
I learned today from the book that in 1969 Christopher Nupen directed and filmed
a performance by an exuberant and youthful quintet of musicians, including
Jacqueline du Pré, of the
<cite>[Piano Quintet in A major, D. 667](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trout_Quintet)</cite>
(given the popular name _The Trout Quintet_, from _Forellenquintett_), by
[Franz Schubert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Schubert).

The film includes footage of the musicians as they arrive in the UK, a portion
of their rehearsal, their backstage antics before and after the performance,
and their complete performance.

The musicans were:

-   [Jacqueline du Pré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_du_Pr%C3%A9), violoncello
-   [Daniel Barenboim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Barenboim), piano
-   [Itzhak Perelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itzhak_Perlman), violin
-   [Pinchas Zukerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinchas_Zukerman), viola
-   [Zubin Mehta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zubin_Mehta), double bass

This is truly a wonderful movie that captures these brilliant musicians early
in their careers. The film communicates the joy of playing chamber music.

The movie is available from
[Allegro Films](https://www.allegrofilms.com/films/the-trout),
but at the time of writing, the DVD is sold out. A bootleg copy of the movie can
be watched on YouTube.

Jacqueline du Pré's career was shortened prematurely in 1973 by
[multiple sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_sclerosis), and
she died in 1987 at the early age of 42. Remarkably, the other four musicians
are still alive.
