I have been listening to episodes of
[The Cello Sherpa Podcast](https://www.thecellosherpa.com/). In the episode
of May 28, 2021, Joel Dallow interviewed his colleague
[Michael Kurth](https://www.aso.org/artists/detail/michael-kurth),
a composer and bassist in the Atlanta Symphony Orchestra. Dallow included some
of Kurth's music in the podcast, and I enjoyed it, causing me to look further.

Michael Kurth's [website](https://kurthmusic.com/) lists his compositions
and recordings.

A video of a live recording of
<cite>[Because They Wave Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SKTxtJL3yWU)</cite>
was released by the Riverside Chamber Players on May 19, 2012.

<cite>[The Music of Michael Kurth: String Quartets](https://music.apple.com/us/album/the-music-of-michael-kurth-string-quartets/641340150)</cite>
(2013), also played by the Riverside Chamber Players, is available from Apple
Music and other streaming services. One of the quartets, "The Boogie Alarm," is
played on the podcast episode, and I like it.

In 2014, ARTS ATL published [Preview: ASO contrabassist Michael Kurth debuts two pieces from his side job as composer](https://www.artsatl.org/preview-aso-contrabassist-michael-kurth-debuts-pieces-side-job-composer/)
about pieces by Kurth that were played by different ensembles.

<cite>[Everything Lasts Forever](https://music.apple.com/us/album/michael-kurth-everything-lasts-forever/1454717664)</cite>
(2019) contains symphonic compositions by Kurth played by the Atlanta
Symphony Orchestra, Robert Spano, conductor.

<cite>[All Will Be Revealed (Live)](https://music.apple.com/us/album/michael-kurth-all-will-be-revealed-live-single/1684057728)</cite>
is a single recorded by the Atlanta Symphony Youth Orchestra in 2023.

Unfortunately, the Riverside Chamber Players disappeared during the Covid-19
pandemic, and the group's website no longer exists.
