Yesterday, June 4, 2026, I had my fourth cello lesson with
[Ana Ospina](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/faculty/ospina)
at
[New School of Music](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org).

## Lesson Notes

Ana wants me to keep my elbow above my bowing hand.

We worked on flexibility of my right wrist when holding the bow; my thumb should
be bent and flexible, and I should be able to flex my fingers, straightening and
bending them to flex my wrist up and down. I am still using the
[Wada Bow Grip](https://wadabowgrip.com)
for comfort and ergonomics.

When playing notes such as C# and D on the A string, I need to press the string
with all lower fingers of my left hand.

We continued working on tunes in
[Blue Book of Tunes - Cello](https://oneworldstrings.com/product/blue-book-of-tunes-a-first-book-for-cello-greene/).
We played together, but my intonation was usually bad. We played the following
tunes:

-   Slow Slithers (pp. 12-13)
-   Songs & Dances (pp. 14-15)
-   Touchdown in D (p. 16)
-   Touchdown in G (p. 17)
-   Twinkle (p. 33)

We also played "Jazz for the Open Strings" by Jiyoung Um using the
[music track on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tjBClWIcO6o).
The cello score is available at [J. W. Pepper](https://www.jwpepper.com).

## Assignments

Practice the tunes we played during the lesson.

Play "Twinkle" on the D, G, and C strings, using pizzicato for the first three
days to learn the fingering of the notes, then bowing.

Begin learning the following tunes in
[Blue Book of Tunes - Cello](https://oneworldstrings.com/product/blue-book-of-tunes-a-first-book-for-cello-greene/):

-   Sad Brother John (p. 17)
-   Close Friends in Dm (p. 18)
-   Close Friends in Gm (p. 19)

Practice "Jazz for the Open Strings."

## Additional Note

I need to squelch my self-critical voice during my lessons. Ana says I'm
progressing very quickly.
