Yesterday, July 10, 2026, I had my eighth cello lesson with
[Ana Ospina](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/faculty/ospina)
at
[New School of Music](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org).

## Lesson Notes

I asked Ana to help me with my posture, holding the cello, and using my
left hand. I need to establish a stable triangle with my knees and the end pin
of the cello without squeezing the cello between my thighs. I need to stretch
my left hand and fingers before playing. I need to keep the fingers of my
left hand on the strings as much as possible.

Ana said I have fingers 2, 3, and 4 in the correct position when I play in first
position; I only need to adjust the position of finger 1. When I played with my
eyes closed, I did much better at positioning my fingers. Ana said to practice
using pizzicato so I don't need to worry about bowing at the same time.

Ana taught me some exercises playing half the D major scale on the D string and
then, without removing my third finger from the string, sliding along the string
to the first, second, and third harmonics, and playing those with a very relaxed
left hand. Repeat this with the other half of the D major scale on the A string.
The idea is this will help me relax my left hand.

We played "French Folk Song" a few times from Suzuki Cello Book 1. I still do
not recognize the notes on the D string when reading music; I get confused from
what I know about reading the treble clef.

The hour passed very quickly; I am always sorry when the lesson comes to an
end, because I would like to keep playing.
