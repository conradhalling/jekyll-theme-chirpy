Today (July 20, 2026) is
[International Moon Day](https://www.un.org/en/observances/moon-day), marking
the first humans on the Moon on July 20, 1969. I remember watching this on TV
when I was 14 years old.

The [American Astronomical Society](https://aas.org)'s
[Committee for the Protection of Astronomy and the Space Environment](https://compasse.aas.org)
(<abbr title="Committee for the Protection of Astronomy and the Space Environment">COMPASSE</abbr>)
held a virtual town hall, "Astronomy on the Moon," today.

> In this 90 minute online event, we will hear from several presenters on
> matters related to astronomy from the Moon.  We will learn about the array of
> cutting-edge astronomical research that could be conducted from the lunar
> surface, possible paths towards protections that could prevent unintended
> interference from sources such as dust, vibration, and electromagnetic
> emissions, and the current status of efforts to engage the international
> community on these issues at venues such as the United Nations Committee on
> the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space (UN COPUOS).

> We will then hear from experts with diverse backgrounds to better understand
> historical, legal, cultural, and societal contexts to consider when planning
> lunar activities.

The first speaker gave a short outline of how we would like to put radio
telescopes on the Moon. He said that astronomers would be developers: they
need to build roads and buildings on the surface of the far side of the Moon.

The next speakers reviewed law in regard to the Moon, including space
treaties. The last speaker spoke about lunar governance and lessons learned
from Antarctica.

I think that enforcement of space treaties and laws will be difficult given the
desire by billionaires and the current U.S. Administration to develop
and mine the Moon (although I predict that it will be difficult to actually make
money on the Moon).

The town hall was recorded; perhaps a link to the recording will be made
available soon on [YouTube](https://youtube.com).
