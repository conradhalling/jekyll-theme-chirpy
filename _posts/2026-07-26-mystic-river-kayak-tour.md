Yesterday (Saturday, July 25, 2026), I participated in a kayak tour of the
Mystic River organized by the
[Mystic River Watershed Association](https://mysticriver.org/) (MRWA).

We congregated at the
[Blessing of the Bay Boathouse](https://mysticriver.org/blessingofthebay)
on the Mystic River shore in Somerville, Massachusetts. MRWA volunteers
provided background information about the boathouse and improvements being
made to the park and adjacent bicycle lanes in the Ten Hills neighborhood of
Somerville.

We paddled out onto the river in kayaks provided by
[Paddle Boston](https://paddleboston.com/). We headed generally
[downstream towards Boston Harbor](https://mysticriver.org/maps),
learning about the
[herring runs](https://mysticriver.org/herring-monitoring),
environmental remediation of the river, the
[Malden River](https://www.maldenriverworks.org/river),
the
[Encore Boston Harbor Hotel & Casino](https://www.encorebostonharbor.com/)
and
[harborwalk](https://www.encorebostonharbor.com/amenities/harborwalk)
on the north shore of the Mystic River,
[removal of invasive plants](https://mysticriver.org/restoring-biodiversity)
from the river, and the
[Amelia Earhart Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amelia_Earhart_Dam).
We passed through the locks of the dam and continued down to the Alford
St. Bridge, where we reversed direction. We had a convenient tailwind that
pushed us up the river to the boathouse.

In previous years I bought a summer pass with
[Paddle Boston](https://paddleboston.com/),
using their
[Kendall Square location](https://paddleboston.com/rentals/our-locations/cambridge-at-kendall-square/)
on the Charles River in Cambridge, Massachusetts, but I let my pass lapse this
year because I had no one to go kayaking with. It was a treat to paddle with
other members of the MRWA.
