It's August, so it's time for the
[Silk Road Global Musician Workshop](https://www.silkroad.org/gmw) here in
Boston. The workshop is organized by cellist
[Mike Block](https://mikeblockmusic.com) and takes place at New England
Conservatory.

The workshop presents four concerts, the first by workshop faculty and the
remaining three by the student participants. I have tickets for all four shows.
Last night's
[Global Musician Showcase](https://www.silkroad.org/gmw-performance-festival),
the faculty performance, was held at
New England Conservatory’s beautiful Jordan Hall at
30 Gainsborough Street, Boston, Massachusetts.

![Pre-performance Mic Check at Jordan Hall](pre-performance-mic-check.png){:width="640" height="480"}_Pre-performance Mic Check at Jordan Hall_

The performers were:

[Mike Block](https://www.silkroad.org/artists-mike-block), GMW Boston Director, cello\
[Eddie Barbash](https://www.eddiebarbash.com), saxophone\
[Hanneke Cassel](https://www.hannekecassel.com), violin\
[Ara Dinkjian](https://www.aradinkjian.com), [oud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oud)\
[Jamey Haddad](https://www.silkroad.org/artists-jamey-haddad), percussion\
[Mei Han 韩梅](https://www.mei-han.com), [zheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guzheng)\
[Balla Kouyaté](https://www.silkroad.org/artists-balla-kouyate), [balafon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balafon)\
[Veronica Robles](https://veronicarobles.com), voice

Mike Block came out on crutches with a brace on his left knee. In his
introduction, Mike said something was different about him: he had a fresh
haircut. After a good laugh from the audience, Mike said he couldn't straighten
his left leg, so he would be forced to sit while playing the cello. Mike, of
course, is the inventor of the
[Block Strap](https://www.cellostrap.com/),
which enables a cellist to stand (and dance) while playing.

Each musician played (or sang) a piece of their own, often supported by one or
more of the other performers. The emphasis was on difference, not similarity, so
we the audience were treated to a wide variety of musical styles, always with a
lot of improvisation. The ninety-minute show ended with music led by Balla
Kouyaté on the balafon, during which the student participants came up on the
stage and danced and sang to the music while the audience stood and danced at
their seats.

I have no concert photos because we were asked not to take photos or record
the concert.
