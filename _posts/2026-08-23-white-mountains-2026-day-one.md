Sunday, August 23, 2026, was the first of six days of camping at
[Lafayette Place Campground](https://www.nhstateparks.org/find-parks-trails/lafayette-place-campground-at-franconia-notch-stat)
in
[Franconia Notch State Park](https://www.nhstateparks.org/find-parks-trails/franconia-notch-state-park)
in the
[White Mountains](https://www.visitwhitemountains.com/)
in New Hampshire.

## Preparations

This was my first trip to the White Mountains after living in
New England for twenty-five years. My plan was to ride my bike, ride two trains,
and do a little hiking.

I had reserved a campsite using
[New Hampshire's reservation system at Reserve America](https://newhampshirestateparks.reserveamerica.com).
My campsite cost me only $35 per night. For late August, campsites were sold
out on Friday and Saturday nights, but availability was good for Sunday through
Thursday nights.

I rented a Zipcar for the six days at a cost of about $700 (gas and insurance
included). I folded down the rear seats and put my Trek Valencia hybrid
bicycle in the back after removing the front rack and pedals. I should have
turned the handlebars to make it easier to get the bike into the back of the
car.

I packed my camping equipment that I use for bicycle touring, including my bear
canister. Instead of freeze-dried meals, which are much lighter when hiking or
touring, I brought cans of tuna fish and Progresso soup to eat for dinners.

## The Drive

The drive was not complicated: I got on I-93 in Somerville, Massachusetts, and
drove north on the interstate to Lafayette Place Campground.

I left at noon. There was some heavy rain on the drive
north on I-93, making driving unsafe near Manchester and Concord, where many
drivers did not reduce their speed even with poor visibility and a lot of
water on the road. I stayed in the right hand lane as much as possible.

Since I was getting sleepy during the drive, I stopped twice. My first stop was
at the New Hampshire I-93 Canterbury Rest Area between Concord and Northfield. I
walked around a little and went inside the visitors center to get advice on what
to see in the White Mountains area; I picked up some maps and brochures.

Farther north on I-93, I stopped a second time at the White Mountains
Attractions Visitors Center in Lincoln, New Hampshire, just east of I-93 on
the Kancamagus Highway.

I had to drive past Lafayette Place Campground on I-93 because there is no
exit when going northbound. I reversed direction a few miles north and drove
back south to the exit. My total driving distance was about 146 miles.

I arrived after three hours at 3:00 p.m.

## Lafayette Place Campground

I set up my campsite and walked around the campground.
The campground was clean and well-maintained, and it was about two-thirds
full. It is located close to I-93, so it suffers from traffic noise. There are
hiking trails in and near the campground, and the Franconia Notch Recreation
Path for walking and bicycling runs just east of the campground.

I found a pedestrian and bicycle tunnel under I-93 that connects the campground
to the trailhead for the Bridle Path Trail and the Falling Waters Trail; these
trails climb more than two thousand feet to the top of Franconia Ridge east of
Franconia Notch.

[AMC](https://outdoors.org) runs a
[daily hiker shuttle](https://www.outdoors.org/shuttle/)
that stops at Lafayette Place Campground when going southbound through Franconia Notch State Park and stops at the Bridle Path and Falling Waters Trailhead, on the east side of I-93 opposite the campground, when going northbound through the state park.

> This shuttle offers a seamless ride from Boston to the backcountry. No car
> swaps. No logistics. Just more time on the trail, and less time in the parking
> lot.

The only shortcoming is that I can't bring my bicycle on the shuttle.

## Franconia Notch Recreation Path

Late in the afternoon, I rode south on the Franconia Notch Recreation Path to
Flume Gorge and back. Going south I lost about 600 feet of elevation, so it was
a good climb coming back. The path is not a rail trail with an even and gentle
grade. Some sections are unreasonably steep.

## Evening Camping Activities

After I ate dinner, there was heavy rain at 7:30 p.m. My tent is excellent, and
I stayed dry. There is no Verizon cell service in campground. I used my Garmin
inReach Messenger to text by satellite. I went to bed at 8:00 p.m.
