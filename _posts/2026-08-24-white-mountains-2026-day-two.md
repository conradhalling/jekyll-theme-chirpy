Monday, August 24, 2026, was the second of six days of camping at
[Lafayette Place Campground](https://www.nhstateparks.org/find-parks-trails/lafayette-place-campground-at-franconia-notch-stat)
in
[Franconia Notch State Park](https://www.nhstateparks.org/find-parks-trails/franconia-notch-state-park)
in the
[White Mountains](https://www.visitwhitemountains.com/)
in New Hampshire. Today my plan was to join a short nature hike, ride the Cog
Railroad to the top of Mt. Washington, and ride my bicycle on the recreation
path.

For the previous day of this six-day camping trip, see
[White Mountains 2026, Day One]( {% link _posts/2026-08-23-white-mountains-2026-day-one.md %} ).

## Lafayette Place Campground

Noisy and inconsiderate neighbors in a nearby campsite woke me up at about
midnight, but my noise-canceling headphones eliminated that problem. I had
brought these and earplugs to deal with traffic noise from nearby I-93, but I
discovered that the traffic noise was not bothering me as much as the noisy
neighbors. Since I was awake, I crawled out of my tent to check the sky. I had a
good view to the north and west, and I could see the bright stars of Cassiopeia,
Cygnus, and Lyra (with Vega); but low moonlight in the southeast washed out the
dimmer stars.

After a good sleep, I got up at 6:00 a.m. and remembered to take a photo of my
campsite. My Zipcar was parked to the left, out of the photo. The site was
much larger than I needed.

## Elephant Head Nature Walk

As for the nature walk, I turned out to be the only person who had registered.
My guide was Mary, who was working full time at AMC during the summer before
going to graduate school in the fall. We walked southeast on Crawford Notch Road
from the Highland Center to the Webster-Jackson Trailhead just past Saco Lake.
We followed that trail past the lake and branched right on the spur to Elephant
Head.

<iframe src="https://ridewithgps.com/embeds?type=trip&id=411914735&sampleGraph=true&privacyCode=wNCV2WJwB14p9zcRdCVy9OaUPGHfAell" style="width: 1px; min-width: 100%; height: 700px; border: none;" scrolling="no"></iframe>

During our walk, Mary showed me four kinds of maple, including
[sugar maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acer_saccharum) and
[red maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acer_rubrum);
[hobblebush](https://nhaudubon.org/plant-profile-hobblebush/), which grows
close to the ground and trips unwary hikers. Mary's sharp eyes spotted a tiny
frog next to the trail and many kinds of fungi. We had a close look at
American beech trees infected with
[beech bark disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beech_bark_disease).

We climbed about 200 feet to the top of Elephant Head, where there was a good
view north.

![Saco Lake, AMC Highland Center, and Mt. Washington Hotel and Resort Viewed from Elephant Head](amc_highland_center_view_from_elephant_head.png){:width="800" height="601" }_Saco Lake, AMC Highland Center, and Mt. Washington Hotel and Resort Viewed from Elephant Head_

I rode the train that departed at noon. We climbed 3,500 feet in 2.8 miles, a
steep climb with an average grade of 24%. The diesel engines are very powerful,
and the cogs, of course, hold the engine on the track to prevent it from sliding
down the rails.

Several photos. Schist with mica in the rocks at the top, not granite.
The backs of the train seats are reversible so you always face in the direction the train is traveling. Back at the base station at about 2:00 p.m.

## Franconia Notch Recreation Path

In the late afternoon, I rode my bike north on the Franconia Notch Recreation
Path.

<iframe src="https://ridewithgps.com/embeds?type=trip&id=411812367&sampleGraph=true&privacyCode=YgqG2kkI0JcFFPIL8vks2JMWJryoA2o6" style="width: 1px; min-width: 100%; height: 700px; border: none;" scrolling="no"></iframe>

As with riding the the southern section, which I had ridden the day before, the
northern section was also very challenging with a few extremely steep hills and
some rough surface. The lowest gear on my Trek Valencia is 18 gear inches, which
was barely sufficient for me to climb the steepest hill.

This description from a twelve-year-old video of riding the recreation path,
[Franconia Notch Bike Trail Virtual Cycling Bike Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SlBRrSOKCX0),
describes the trail succinctly and humorously:

> Speed varies greatly due to the nature of the trail, basically the complete
> opposite of a rail trail. I think if you go up a little further north there
> might even be a vertical loop like in the old Hot Wheels tracks.

I turned around when I reached an old auto bridge over a stream, with barriers
blocking automobile drivers but not bicyclists or hikers. I lacked a good map
and a cell phone signal, and I was disoriented and not sure where I was when I
turned around. The bridge seemed like a possible remnant of old U.S. Route 3
that had been replaced by the newer I-93 route.

When I had access to a good map a couple of days later, I learned that the
bridge crosses Lafayette Brook and that the Franconia Notch Recreation Trail
continues for a few more miles northward. I also discovered later that there was
a good cell phone signal at the bridge. And the parking lot near the bridge is
accessed from an exit from northbound I-93 and is located at the trailhead of
Franconia Snomo, a snowmobile trail.

I returned to the campground by 5:30 p.m. I did a little walking around the
campground, made dinner, cleaned up, and was in bed at dark, 8:00 p.m.
