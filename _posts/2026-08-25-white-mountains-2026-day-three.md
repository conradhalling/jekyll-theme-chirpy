Tuesday, August 25, 2026, was the third of six days of camping at Lafayette
Place Campground in Franconia Notch State Park in the White Mountains of New
Hampshire. Today I drove the scenic Kancamagus Highway from Lincoln to Conway,
rode the Conway Scenic Railroad, did a little hiking in Crawford Notch, and
bicycled on the Franconia Notch Recreation Path.

For the previous day of this six-day camping trip, see
[White Mountains 2026, Day Two]( {% link _posts/2026-08-24-white-mountains-2026-day-two.md %} ).

## Morning Campsite Activities

Once again, I had noisy neighbors as late as 1:30 a.m. (Enforcement of the noise
rule is nil at this campground.) I was wearing my noise-canceling headphones, so
I noticed only when I got up for a nature break and to look at the stars. As
with the night before, the moon was low in the southeast, and I could see
Cassiopeia, Cygnus, and Lyra.

I got up at 5:30 a.m., ate breakfast, and organized my stuff for the day.

## Conway Scenic Railroad

I left the campground at 6:30 a.m. to drive east to the
[Conway Scenic Railroad](https://www.conwayscenic.com/) in North Conway.

I drove south on I-93 and Route 3 to Lincoln, then east on the Kancamagus
Highway from Lincoln to Conway, and north on U.S. 302 to the railroad station
in North Conway. The early morning sunlight sometimes made it hard to see.
I arrived very eary at 7:50 a.m. for a train scheduled at 9:00 a.m.

![Train Station](train_station.png){:width="800" height="600"}_Train Station_

I walked around North Conway, which is touristy (it's a big ski town in the
winter). I picked up my ticket at 8:20 a.m. and boarded the train at about
8:45 a.m.

I paid extra to ride in the top of the dome car of the
[Mountaineer route](https://www.conwayscenic.com/excursions/notch-train/).
This description from the website is accurate:

> Conway Scenic Railroad’s most scenic excursion, the Mountaineer evokes the
> elegance of 1950s American rail travel. This 4- to 4.5-hour round trip winds
> through New Hampshire’s breathtaking Crawford Notch, offering guests a
> condensed yet memorable experience of classic transcontinental train travel.

The ride was slow, especially at the beginning where the cars rocked side to
side on the old tracks. We were given a narration that I found interesting,
but other passengers complained that the narrator's tone of voice made them
sleepy. The route was flanked by trees, and we traveled through green tunnels.
We leared that in the early 1900s, New Hampshire was only 20% forested after
the land had been cleared for farming, but now it is 80% forested.

The route followed the Saco River upstream into Crawford Notch. The mile posts
along the tracks measure the distance from Portland, Maine. Whistle signs mark
the route with a W, alerting the engineer to blow the whistle for a road
crossing; the whistle pattern is long, long, short, long. I took a few photos
during the ride, but they were spoiled by reflections from the glass of the
dome.

After about 80 minutes, we arrived at Crawford Notch Depot adjacent to the
AMC Highland Center and Saco Lake in Crawford Notch.

![Crawford Notch Depot](crawford_notch_depot.png){:width="800" height="600}_Crawford Notch Depot_

We spent 50 minutes at Crawford Notch Depot, where the engineer moved the
engine to the opposite end of the train for the return back.

![Train at Crawford Notch Depot](train_at_crawford_notch_depot.png){:width="800" height="329}_Train at Crawford Notch Depot_

I walked over to Saco Lake and photographed flowers on the west side of the road.

The skies were cloudy for the slow ride back to Conway. I watched the scenery,
listened to conversations, and ate my lunch (a sandwich, whoopie pie, potato
chips, and a bottle of water). We arrived back at North Conway at 12:53 p.m.

## Exploring the Valley East of Mt. Washington

From North Conway, I drove north on State Route 16 east of Mt. Washington
towards Gorham, following the
[Ellis River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellis_River_(New_Hampshire)).
At 1:45 p.m., I stopped for half an hour at the Glen Ellis Scenic Area and
walked to Glen Ellis Falls.

I continued driving north to the AMC Pinkham Notch Visitor Center, located
on the Appalachian Trail. I walked south to
the Lost Pond Trail and walked south to Lost Pond. Total distance maybe 1.3
miles. I went into the center and bought some more AMC maps of the
White Mountains.

I walked 0.5 mile of AT, then turned around. Beaver dam. Photo.

I drove to Gorham on Route 16, then west on U.S. Route 2. I found the trailhead
of the Presidential Rail Trail a little west of Gorham.

I had a long drive back because I took U.S. Route 2 to State Route 115 to U.S.
Route 3. But at the intersection with U.S. Route 302, I was confused by the
signs, and I turned right onto U.S. Route 302 instead of continuing south on
U.S. Route 3. This was the long way around to I-93, maybe ten extra miles.

## Evening Camping Activities

At 6:00 p.m., I rode my bicycle on all of the campground loop roads, then
through the tunnel under I-93 to trailhead and parking area. I returned to the
recreation path next to the campground and rode easy distances both ways, for a
total of 4.04 miles, returning at 6:30 p.m.

I ate canned soup and tuna for dinner, after which I washed the cans for
recycling. I brushed my teeth and cleaned my cooking pot. I was trying to keep
any food smells away from my camp in case of bear activity. I walked around the
campground loop roads. I organized my equipment. While driving, I had used the
car to charge my phone; I charged my other electronics using my energy banks. I
studied the maps I had bought today to figure out exactly where I had been. I
went to bed at 8:30 p.m.
