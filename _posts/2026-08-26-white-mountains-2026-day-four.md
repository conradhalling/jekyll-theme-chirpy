Wednesday, August 26, 2026, was the fourth of six days of camping at
[Lafayette Place Campground](https://www.nhstateparks.org/find-parks-trails/lafayette-place-campground-at-franconia-notch-stat)
in
[Franconia Notch State Park](https://www.nhstateparks.org/find-parks-trails/franconia-notch-state-park)
in the
[White Mountains](https://www.visitwhitemountains.com/)
in New Hampshire. Today I hiked and bicycled in Franconia Notch State Park.

For the previous day of this six-day camping trip, see
[White Mountains 2026, Day Three]( {% link _posts/2026-08-25-white-mountains-2026-day-three.md %} ).

## Camping Notes

When I got up at 12:30 a.m., the moon was shining brightly. I could find Lyra,
Cygnus, and Cassiopeia, but I couldn't find Polaris and the Little Dipper. None
of my astronomy apps on my phone would work because I did not have a cell
signal.

I slept well until 6:00 a.m., with no noisy neighbors. The campground was only
about a third full, pretty surprising for late August with nice weather. I was
tired of driving after the past three days, and I did not drive today.

## Hike in Franconia Notch

In the morning, I left for a nearly six-hour hike, starting with a climb up the
Lonesome Lake Trail from Lafayette Place Campground to the
[AMC Lonesome Lake hut](https://www.outdoors.org/destinations/new-hampshire/lonesome-lake-hut/)
on the Appalachian Trail (AT). In the White Mountains, the individual trails
that coincide with the AT have their own names. I continued southeast down the
Cascade Brook Trail (AT) to the
[Basin](https://4000footers.com/basin.shtml)
of the
[Pemigewasset River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pemigewasset_River).
I turned north on the Pemi Trail back to the campground for a total recorded
distance of 8.53 miles.

I left my campsite at 8:00 a.m. to climb the Lonesome Lake Trail from the
campground to Lonesome Lake and the AMC hut on the AT.

![Lonesome Lake Trailhead in Lafayette Place Campground](lonesome_lake_trailhead.png){:width="600" height="800" }_Lonesome Lake Trailhead in Lafayette Place Campground_

After hiking 0.7 mile, I branched left at the junction with the Hi-Cannon Trail,
continuing on the Lonesome Lake Trail.

![Junction of Lonesome Lake Trail with Hi-Cannon Trail](lonesome_lake_trail_junction.png){:width="800" height="600" }_Junction of Lonesome Lake Trail with Hi-Cannon Trail_

The total climb to the lake and hut was about 900 feet, starting at about 1,800
ft and climbing to 2,700 feet. The trail was very steep and rocky in places.

![Lonesome Lake Trail](lonesome_lake_trail.png){:width="600" height="800" }_Lonesome Lake Trail_

The day was cloudy, so Lonesome Lake was gray and uninviting.

![Lonesome Lake](lonesome_lake.png){:width="800" height="600" }_Lonesome Lake_

At Lonesome Lake, Lonesome Lake Trail intersected with the Cascade Brook
Trail, with the Fishin' Jimmy Trail (AT) branching to the right and continuing
past the AMC hut.

![David A. Copestakes Memorial Plaque Near Lonesome Lake](david_a_copestakes_memorial_plaque.png){:width="800" height="600" }_David A. Copestakes Memorial Plaque Near Lonesome Lake_

![Boardwalk Near Lonesome Lake](boardwalk_near_lonesome_lake.png){:width="600" height="800" }_Boardwalk Near Lonesome Lake_

![Trail Stairs to AMC Lonesome Lake Hut](trail_stairs_to_lonesome_lake_hut.png){:width="600" height="800" }_Trail Stairs to AMC Lonesome Lake Hut_

I briefly went into the AMC hut to check it out, and there were four or five
through-hikers relaxing inside. I was tempted by the tasty baked goods for sale,
but I decided I should leave them for the through-hikers.

Descending from the hut, I followed the Fishin' Jimmy Trail a short distance
back to Lonesome Lake and the junction with the Cascade Brook Trail (AT), where
I turned right to descend the Cascade Brook Trail towards the Pemigewasset
River.

![Junction of Fishin' Jimmy Trail with Cascade Brook Trail](fishin_jimmy_trail_and_cascade_brook_trail_junction.png){:width="800" height="600" }_Junction of Fishin' Jimmy Trail with Cascade Brook Trail_

I followed the Cascade Brook Trail for about 2.8 miles, passing the junction
to the Kinsman Pond Trail and crossing two streams.

![Junction of Cascade Brook Trail with Kinsman Pond Trail](kinsman_pond_trail_junction.png){:width="800" height="600" }_Junction of Cascade Brook Trail with Kinsman Pond Trail_

![Stream Crossing](stream_crossing.png){:width="800" height="600" }_Stream Crossing_

I continued on the Cascade Brook Trail under the I-93 bridges to the Franconia
Notch Recreation Path. I went that way rather than branching left onto the
Basin-Cascades Trail so I could walk as much of the AT as possible because I
would someday like to hike sections of the AT. I turned left (north) onto the
Franconia Notch Recreation Path (AT), crossing the Pemigewasset River over a
graceful bridge.

![Recreation Path Bridge Over the Pemigewasset River](recreation_path_bridge.png){:width="800" height="600" }_Recreation Path Bridge Over the Pemigewasset River_

I continued north on the recreation path to the junction with the Libery Spring
Trail (AT), which branched off to the right to climb to the top of Franconia
Ridge. At this point, I was passed by a single through-hiker heading north bound
on the AT.

![Liberty Spring Trailhead](liberty_spring_trailhead.png){:width="800" height="600" }_Liberty Spring Trailhead_

I continued walking north on the Franconia Notch Recreation Path to The Basin
of the [Pemigewasset River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pemigewasset_River).
I explored The Basin and the nearby Baby Flume of the Pemigewasset River,
walking all the little trails and taking a few photos, and I ate a snack.

![Franconia Notch Recreation Path](franconia_notch_recreation_path.png){:width="800" height="600" }_Franconia Notch Recreation Path_

![Baby Flume of the Pemigewasset River Near The Basin](baby_flume.png){:width="800" height="600}_Baby Flume of the Pemigewasset River Near The Basin_

![The Basin](the_basin.png){:width="800" height="600}_The Basin_

![Pemigewasset River Near The Basin](pemi_river_near_the_basin.png){:width="800" height="600}_Pemigewasset River Near The Basin_

![Pemigewasset River Upstream of The Basin](pemi_river_upstream_of_the_basin.png){:width="800" height="600}_Pemigewasset River Upstream of The Basin_

The Pemigewasset River has its source at Profile Lake in Franconia Notch and
flows south into the Merrimac River in Massachusetts and thence to the Atlantic
Ocean. The
[Saco River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saco_River),
which flows from its source at Saco Lake in Crawford Notch only a few miles to
the east, and which I saw when visiting the AMC Highland Center on Monday and
riding the Conway Scenic Railway on Tuesday, flows in a southeast direction into
Maine and finally into the Atlantic Ocean north of the mouth of the Merrimac
River.

Near The Basin, I found the junction of the Pemi Trail with the Basin-Cascade
Trail. The Basin-Cascade Trail heads northwest up to join the Cascade Brook
Trail (AT) that I had descended earlier in the day. The Pemi Trail runs along
the Pemigewasset River, in the same direction as the Franconia Notch
Recreation Trail.

![Junction of the Basin-Cascades and Pemi Trails](junction_of_the_basin_cascades_and_pemi_trails.png){:width="800" height="600}_Junction of the Basin-Cascades and Pemi Trails_

![Pemi Trail Sign](pemi_trail_sign.png){:width="800" height="600}_Pemi Trail Sign_

I hiked two miles north on the Pemi Trail, which was a little overgrown, gaining
about 300 feet in elevation, and I arrived at Lafayette Place Campground at 1:45
p.m. When I reached my campsite, I had hiked 8.53 miles in 5 hours 45 minutes.

## Bicycle Ride in Franconia Notch

At 4:15 p.m., I left on my bicycle to ride north on the
[Franconia Notch Recreation Path](https://www.traillink.com/trail/franconia-notch-recreation-path/).

<iframe src="https://ridewithgps.com/embeds?type=trip&id=412909867&sampleGraph=true&privacyCode=PEAKBeq2etWgUFDY8RDwlrLhTxPwQDTT" style="width: 1px; min-width: 100%; height: 700px; border: none;" scrolling="no"></iframe>

I took my time to explore the
[Old Man of the Mountain Profile Plaza and Historic Site](https://www.oldmannh.org/profiler_plaza.php),
the exterior of the nearby Old Man Museum (which had closed for the day before I
arrived), the retired
[Aerial Tramway](https://www.cannonmt.com/attractions/aerial-tramway)
to the top of Cannon Mountain, and the nearby
[New England Ski Museum](https://www.cannonmt.com/new-england-ski-museum)
(also closed for the day).

I continued north around
[Profile Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profile_Lake_(New_Hampshire))
to a park with a swimming area and some RV campsites.

![Cliffs Near Profile Lake](cliffs_near_profile_lake.png){:width="800" height="600}_Cliffs Near Profile Lake_

I returned to the
recreation path and rode north to the end at
[Skookumchuck Trailhead](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r09/whitemountain/recreation/skookumchuck-trailhead),
for a distance so far of 7.43 miles.

At this point, my Apple Watch had only 3% charge left and I turned it off.
Don't try to track too many activities in one day with an Apple Watch.

I discovered I had a strong cell signal, so I took advantage to do a little
texting and download a star charts app. From Skookumchuck Trailhead, I rode
south, returning to the campground, for 5.30 more miles, giving me a total of
12.93 miles for the ride.

I admired the landscaping in Franconia Notch State Park. The following three
views sparked my imagination in which I visualized wide, nicely groomed roads
devoted exclusively to non-motorized transportation such as bicycles with
adjacent grassy fields for picnicking and camping. I imagine a network of
such trails spanning North America.
