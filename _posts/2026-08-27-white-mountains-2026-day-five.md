Thursday, August 27, 2026, was the fifth of six days of camping at
[Lafayette Place Campground](https://www.nhstateparks.org/find-parks-trails/lafayette-place-campground-at-franconia-notch-stat)
in
[Franconia Notch State Park](https://www.nhstateparks.org/find-parks-trails/franconia-notch-state-park)
in the
[White Mountains](https://www.visitwhitemountains.com/)
in New Hampshire. Today I hiked on the Falling Waters Trail east of the
campground, and I rode south on the Franconia Notch Recreation Path.

For the previous day of this six-day camping trip, see
[White Mountains 2026, Day Four]( {% link _posts/2026-08-26-white-mountains-2026-day-four.md %} ).

## Morning Activities

I fell asleep before 8:30 last night and slept until 2:30 a.m. There was a
full moon and some clouds, so I couldn't see many stars. I did a little reading,
and I listened to my audiobook until I fell asleep again at 4:30 a.m. I woke
up at dawn, 5:30 a.m.

## Hike

I thought I would try to hike to Franconia Ridge today. The day was sunny, not
too warm, with a predicted high in the 70s °F. I consulted the list of the ten
essential items on my
[New Hampshire Hike Safe card](https://www.wildlife.nh.gov/get-outside/hiking-safety)
and made sure I had all of them.

Leaving the campground at 8:00 a.m., I walked through the tunnel under I-93 to
the trailhead for the Falling Waters Trail. I hiked up the trail, bearing right
where the Old Bridle Path branched off to the left. I hiked slowly and with
occasional difficulty past Swiftwater Falls up to Cloudland Falls at about 2,600
feet elevation.

But the trail was too difficult for me, growing increasingly steep and slippery,
and it was another 2,100 feet of climbing to reach Little Haystack Mountain. I
needed better hiking shoes, and I needed my trekking poles, which were sitting
in the living room at home. I reversed direction and descended to the
campground, arriving at 10:15 a.m. My total distance was 3.41 miles, according
to my watch.

## Midday Camping Activities

I washed my hiking socks because my feet, socks, and shoes were
very stinky. I took a shower (with no soap because I had forgotten to bring
any). Four quarters bought five minutes of hot water, which was plenty. I
cleaned my feet again and the inside of my shoes with Wet Ones before putting
on fresh hiking socks.

I had just enough food for the rest of the trip. I had left the cheese in trunk
of the car yesterday, and the trunk got warm from the sun, so I threw out the
cheese. I organized my equipment and supplies while charging my phone from an
energy bank. I lay in my tent, listening to cello music, and I took a nap for at
least thirty minutes until 3:30 p.m.

## Bicycle Ride

I changed into bicycle clothes and rode my bike at 3:45–5:45 p.m. down the
Franconia Notch Recreation Path and on past the Flume parking lot to North
Lincoln, where there were some nice motels and also some sad summer businesses
that were seemingly not open. I turned around at a junction with I-93 after
riding 6.4 downhill miles and began the long, long climb back up the notch to
the campground. Before I reached the Basin, I spotted where the Liberty Spring
Trail, also the Appalachian Trail, branched off east from the recreation
path to climb to Franconia Ridge. My total ride was about 12.8 miles.

## Evening Camping Activities

It looked like it might rain, so I put all nonessential items in the trunk of
the car. There were a few sprinkles. I ate my last can of soup with wheat
crackers and peanut butter. I cleaned up, slid into my sleeping bag at 7:54
p.m., and read until I fell asleep before thirty minutes had passed.

I did not drive for the second day in a row.
