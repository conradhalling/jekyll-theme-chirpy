Friday, August 28, 2026, was the last of six days of camping at
[Lafayette Place Campground](https://www.nhstateparks.org/find-parks-trails/lafayette-place-campground-at-franconia-notch-stat)
in
[Franconia Notch State Park](https://www.nhstateparks.org/find-parks-trails/franconia-notch-state-park)
in the
[White Mountains](https://www.visitwhitemountains.com/)
in New Hampshire.

Today I rode my bicycle to the north end of the recreation path before packing
up and driving home.

For the previous day of this six-day camping trip, see
[White Mountains 2026, Day Five]( {% link _posts/2026-08-27-white-mountains-2026-day-five.md %} ).

## Camping Activities

I got up at 5:30 a.m. when the sky was just becoming light. It was going to be a
beautiful day. To stretch my legs, I walked all campground loop roads from 5:40
a.m. to 6:15 a.m.

I began packing while eating breakfast.

## Bicycle Ride

For my last ride of this trip, I rode north on the
[Franconia Notch Recreation Path](https://www.traillink.com/trail/franconia-notch-recreation-path/)
to the northern terminus at Skookumchuck Trailhead. This route had become very
familiar to me since the beginning of this trip.

<iframe src="https://ridewithgps.com/embeds?type=trip&id=417009906&sampleGraph=true&privacyCode=kD3cSjiBvhwQ8g46VjyRrkiJUHykIlG0" style="width: 1px; min-width: 100%; height: 700px; border: none;" scrolling="no"></iframe>

I took photos on the ride back to the campground.

![Skookumchuck Trailhead](skookumchuck_trailhead.png){:width="800" height="600"}_Skookumchuck Trailhead_

![Corridor 11 Trail, a Snowmobile Road, 0.26 Mile South of Skookumchuck Trailhead](corridor_11.png){:width="800" height="600"}_Corridor 11 Trail, a Snowmobile Road, 0.26 Mile South of Skookumchuck Trailhead_

The grassy wayside shown below, 0.86 mile from the north end of the recreation
path, made me imagine waysides like this spaced at twenty-kilometer intervals
along bicycle trails in North America where touring bicyclists and hikers could
camp. Each wayside would provide water, sanitary facilities, and electricity.
Twenty kilometers is a good daily distance for hikers. Perhaps undeveloped
waysides could be provided at five-kilometer intervals.

![Grassy Wayside](grassy_wayside.png){:width="800" height="600"}_Grassy Wayside_

The photo below shows what a cluster of tents at such a wayside might look like
(these were tents I photographed in Lafayette Place Campground).

![Cluster of Tents](cluster_of_tents.png){:width="800" height="638"}_Cluster of Tents_

I took more photos while riding south back to the campground. The old highway
bridge, now blocked from automobile traffic, is part of the recreation path
where it crosses Lafayette Brook.

![Old Highway Bridge with Mt. Lafayette in the Background](old_highway_bridge.png){:width="800" height="600"}_Old Highway Bridge with Mt. Lafayette in the Background_

![Ski Trails and Chairlift Near Echo Lake](ski_trails.png){:width="800" height="451"}_Ski Trails and Chairlift Near Echo Lake_

![Recreation Path Just South of Echo Lake](recreation_path.png){:width="800" height="600"}_Recreation Path Just South of Echo Lake_

I arrived back at my campsite at 9:08 a.m. after riding 10.72 hilly miles.

## Drive Home

The day was warm and sunny. I finished packing and left at 10:45 a.m., fifteen
minutes before check-out time at 11:00 a.m. The drive was simple: I got on I-93
and drove to Somerville, Massachusetts, where I exited I-93. I got home at
1:15 p.m.
