I want to make video recordings of myself while I practice playing my cello, but
my current phone, an Apple iPhone 16 Pro, has only 128 GB of storage, and it is
usually full since I store many photos, music tracks, and podcasts, among other
things. I decided to trade in this phone, which I purchased twenty months ago in
December 2024, on an iPhone 17 Pro, for which the base model has 256 GB of
storage.

I could wait a few more weeks to buy an iPhone 18 Pro model, which Apple will
announce seven days from this post on September 9, 2026, in an event called
"Surprise and shine." But if I recall correctly from what has happened in the
past, after the announcement of the new models Apple will immediately stop
selling the discontinued iPhone 17 Pro and iPhone 17 Pro Max models. There were
will a few days to wait before customers are able to order the iPhone 18 Pro
models, and customers will wait a week or more for the new phones to be
delivered. Usually the delays grow as early demand exceeds supply.

Furthermore, because of RAMageddon—the shortage of RAM chips caused by the
expansion of AI companies—the iPhone 18 Pro models are predicted by many
analysts to cost $100 to $300 more than the equivalent iPhone 17 Pro models.

Since feature improvements of new iPhones are now small and incremental, it is
more important to me to save a hundred dollars or more on my new phone than to
buy the latest iteration. The iPhone 17 Pro costs $1,099; I will trade in my old
phone, for which Apple says I should receive a $630 credit.

With all this in mind, I ordered a new iPhone 17 Pro today at about 10:30 a.m.,
and I picked it up at 3:15 p.m. at the local Apple store in Cambridge,
Massachusetts. Instead of a black silicone case like I purchased for my iPhone
16 Pro, I ordered a green TechWoven case for the new phone. I hope the green
color will make it easier to find my phone when I set it on dark furniture. The
silicone case has been a good case for protecting my phone from occasional
drops, but it sometimes makes it difficult to get my phone out of my pants
pocket.

As I write, it is now 6:30 p.m., and I have successfully moved into the new
phone except for most of the 240 podcast episodes I have in my Overcast queue.
Apple makes it very easy to transfer data and passwords from an old phone to a
new phone and to activate the new phone with my Verizon account. (Verizon
charged $40 for the account transfer to the new phone, because they can.)

So far, with about an hour of use, the new phone feels nearly identical to the
old phone. Because of the wide camera plateau, the iPhone 17 Pro does not rock
on a hard surface like the iPhone 16 Pro does. But with the TechWoven case, the
new phone slides much more easily on a smooth surface than the old phone with
the silicone case.

In about fourteen months, after the folding iPhone has been on the market
for a year, I might order one. I can wait a year to learn if the folding model
is durable and retains its value since it is predicted to be very expensive
(upwards of $2,000).
