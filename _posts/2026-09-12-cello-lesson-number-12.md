Yesterday, September 11, 2026, I had my twelfth cello lesson with
[Ana Ospina](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/faculty/ospina)
at
[New School of Music](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org).

## Practice Session Notes

From <cite>Suzuki Cello School Volume 1</cite>, I practiced “Allegro" (including
playing with a backing track), "Tonalization," D scale, "May Song," "Song of the
Wind," "O Come, Little Children," and "Perpetual Motion."

From <cite>Blue Book of Tunes</cite>  I practiced "See Saw" melody and harmony,
"Sad Brother John," "Slow Slithers," and "Songs & Dances."

I practiced scales, sometimes playing with drones (which is very relaxing).

I practiced playing softly.

This morning I went to
[Luthier Collective](https://www.evabroadwaystrings.com)
to try bows with Mariia Gorkun, and I have a bow on loan for two weeks to try
out. Eva Broadway was also there, and I ordered replacement strings for my
cello.

A couple of days ago the bunion pad came off the back of my cello; Ana had put
it there two weeks ago as a landmark for my thumb. I wiped the glue off with my
microfiber cleaning cloth.

## Lesson Notes

Ana tried the bow I have on loan, and she said she likes it.

Ana had me play arpeggios in D major, C major, and G major. Then she had me
play scales in the same three keys.

I need to try to connect my notes better; my bowing was awkward during the
lesson. String crossings are a problem for me; Ana tried to improve this, but I
didn't have the coordination to do it right.

From <cite>Suzuki Cello School Volume 1</cite>, we played:

-   "May Song" (Ana said I'm done with this)
-   "Allegro" (I have played this better at home)
-   "Perpetual Motion" in D (very rough)

Ana introduced me to "Long, Long Ago." From <cite>Blue Book of Tunes</cite>,
I sight-read "Bingo".
