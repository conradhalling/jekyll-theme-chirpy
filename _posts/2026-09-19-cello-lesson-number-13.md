Yesterday, September 18, 2026, I had my thirteenth cello lesson with
[Ana Ospina](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/faculty/ospina)
at
[New School of Music](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org).

## Practice Session Notes

For the past week I practiced "Long, Long Ago" and "Allegretto" from
<cite>Suzuki Cello School Volume 1</cite>. These pieces are teaching me the
notes on the G string and the D on the C string. After a few days, I began
playing both pieces with backing tracks at 50% to 70% performance tempo, but not
very cleanly. The backing tracks are slowly helping me correct my intonation.

For one day, I also practiced "Bingo" from <cite>Blue Book of Tunes</cite>.

## Lesson Notes

Ana and I played "Long, Long Ago," "Allegretto," and "Bingo" together, with
Ana playing the accompaniment. I asked Ana to demonstrate how to play the
accented notes in "Allegretto," because I've heard different interpretations
in YouTube videos. Ana demonstrated how to play the accented notes and had me
practice.

For "Allegretto," Ana demonstrated ways of breaking up the piece into note
clusters to change the rhythm and introduce variation. I have read about this
approach in books about learning music, but I have never tried it before. Ana
demonstrated playing the two eighth notes, the quarter note, then pausing for a
beat, playing the entire piece that way. That makes it possible to focus on a
cluster of three notes at a time with a pause before playing the next cluster. I
played "Allegretto" that way. Then Ana demonstrated grouping the notes into
clusters of six (eighth eighth quarter eighth eighth quarter) with a beat
between clusters and had me play "Allegretto" that way, too. (That makes the
rhythm 5/4, which is interesting.)

After listening to many podcasts about playing string instruments, such
as
[The Cello Sherpa]( {% link _posts/2026-05-24-the-cello-sherpa.md %} ),
[Lonely Cello]( {% link _posts/2026-08-20-lonely-cello-podcast.md %}),
[Stand Partners for Life]( {%link _posts/2026-06-14-stand-partners-for-life.md %}),
and
[Mind Over Finger]( {% link _posts/2026-07-05-mind-over-finger-podcast.md %}),
I am a big believer in playing scales. Ana had me play one-octave D major and G
major scales. Then we played the D major scale together as a round, where I
started first (D, E, ...) and Ana came in second with the same scale as I was
continuing with the F#, G, ..., so we were playing thirds while ascending. Ana
had me play a single D at the top of the scale and begin descending. When I
reached the open D at the bottom of the scale, Ana had me repeat that note until
after she had reached the same note with her scale. Then we reversed, with Ana
starting first and me coming in two beats later.

Finally, Ana introduced to me the piece "It's Raining" from <cite>Blue Book of
Tunes</cite>, teaching me how to play the rhythmic harmony. Then we turned to
"Ode to Joy" on p. 35, where Ana asked me to play the melody. This piece has
slurs, and I ignored every one!

As always, that was a challenging but enjoyable lesson, and I left inspired to
work on new things for the next two weeks.
