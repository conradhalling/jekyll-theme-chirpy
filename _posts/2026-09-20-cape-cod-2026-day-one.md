Sunday, September 20, 2026, was the first of five days of camping at
[Shawme-Crowell State Forest](https://www.mass.gov/locations/shawme-crowell-state-forest)
in Sandwich, Massachusetts, on Cape Cod.

I like to visit Cape Cod on weekdays after Labor Day. The weather is still nice, businesses
are still open, but the crowds have disappeared and the campgrounds are not
crowded. See, for example,
[How to experience ‘second summer’ on Cape Cod right now, according to locals](https://www.boston.com/travel/cape-cod-travel/2026/09/18/how-to-experience-second-summer-on-cape-cod/).

On this trip, I camped at Shawme-Crowell State Forest, which is located in
Sandwich on the Upper Cape near the Cape Cod Canal. This state forest contains
700 acres of pitch pine and scrub oak forest with sandy soil, and the campground
is large with over 200 sites. I had never stayed at this campground before.

## Preparations

I took my Trek hybrid bicycle with me. I used a pedal wrench to remove the
pedals and a hex key to loosen the bolts that secure the handlebar to the stem
so I could turn the handlebars ninety degrees. These preparations made it easier
to put my bicycle in the back deck of the Honda Civic hatchback I had rented
from Zipcar.

I took all of my camping equipment that I use when touring by bicycle. I planned
to buy canned food instead of carrying dehydrated backpacking food since I
didn't neet to worry about weight.

## The Drive

I left the house at 12:30 p.m. on a Sunday. I stopped right away at the local
gas station to put air in front right tire. I put in a lot of air, but not
enough to reset sensor. Zipcar maintenance should have taken care of this
before I used the car; I think Zipcar's maintenance is declining.

Traffic, as always, was slow through
Boston until I got farther south on I-93, after which I was able to drive the
speed limit all the way to the Sagamore Bridge crossing the Cape Cod Canal. When
I reached the Cape, State Route 6A and and U.S. Route 6 on the Cape side of the
Sagamore Bridge were clogged with weekend traffic exiting the Cape. I arrived
at the campground at about 2:30 p.m.

## Afternoon Activities

I set up my campsite by 3:25 in moderate rain, 62 °F. The campground was mostly
empty after the weekend campers had left. The rain was annoying, but the
forecast was for it to end the next day.

![My Campsite at Shawme-Crowell State Forest](campsite.png){:width="800" height="600"}_My Campsite at Shawme-Crowell State Forest_

![My Zipcar, a Honda Civic Hatchback](zipcar.png){:width="800" height="600"}_My Zipcar, a Honda Civic Hatchback_

I walked more than a mile to the campground entrance, and then I tried to walk
on State Route 130 to the intersection with State Route 6A. These are typical
narrow Cape Cod roads, built with no consideration for walkers or bicyclists. I
did not see a way that I could walk or bicycle to one of the local grocery
stores (Market Basket or Stop & Shop). I returned to my campsite, having walked
4.21 miles measured with my watch.

I drove to the local Market Basket the hard way, taking a wrong route that
took me north over the Sagamore Bridge. I exited Route 6 to the road along the
north side of the Cape Cod Canal and figured out how to get back over the bridge
to the grocery store, where I bought a turkey wrap, cookies, and five cans of
Progresso soup.

Back at my campsite, I ate a can of soup, the turkey wrap, and a cookie for
dinner. An owl began hooting as it got dark. The park was very quiet, and I
could not hear traffic noise from nearby U.S. Route 6. I cleaned my spoon and
the soup can, brushed my teeth, set up my sleep system, and was asleep in my bag
at 7:30 p.m.

I woke up at 9:15 p.m. to the sound of rain; I was too warm in my
sleeping bag, and I took off my socks. After listening to an audiobook for a
couple of hours, I fell back asleep.
