Monday, September 21, 2026, was the second of five days of camping at
[Shawme-Crowell State Forest](https://www.mass.gov/locations/shawme-crowell-state-forest)
in Sandwich, Massachusetts, on Cape Cod.

For the previous day of this five-day camping trip, see
[Cape Cod 2026, Day One]( {% link _posts/2026-09-20-cape-cod-2026-day-one.md %} ).

## Morning Activities

I slept 11:30–5:00 with short interruptions. It rained periodically overnight
but stopped at about 7:00 a.m. I explored the empty campsites in the local loop
(only three sites out of about twenty were occupied). I made and ate breakfast
(granola, muesli, and cocoa mix; two cookies).

I installed the pedals on my bike and straightened the handlebars. I rode all
paved roads in the campground, about 4.8 miles. There are some very steep (but
short) hills where I used my lowest gear, and this became a HIIT workout. My
fitness was not what I wished.

## Heritage Museums & Gardens

Looking at a map, I discovered the campground was close to
[Heritage Museums & Gardens](https://heritagemuseumsandgardens.org/). I walked
over using forest trails, less than a mile.

The grounds contain many kinds of trees with good labels.

![Bark of Japanese Stewartia](bark.png){:width="600" height="800}_Bark of Japanese Stewartia_

Cape Cod was inhabited by the Wampanoag peoples before European colonists
arrived. The gardens contain a mush8n made from a white pine tree. From a
sign:

>   MUSH8N: Water—whether that be ponds, rivers, or oceans—plays a key part in
>   Wampanoag life. In the pre-contact era, people usually traveled by foot or
>   by *mush8n* (pronounced mishoon), which means "boat." This 12–foot mush8n is
>   suitable for two people, while much larger ones could hold up to forty
>   people. With rivers as highways, Wampanoag people could use a mush8n to
>   fish, travel, and interact with other tribes. This mush8n was made by
>   Wampanoag culture-keepers who work to research, maintain, and pass on their
>   tribe's traditions to the next generations.
>
>   WÔPANÂAK "People of the first light": The Wôpanâak (or Wampanoag in English)
>   have lived in what is now Southeastern Massachusetts and Rhode Island for
>   more than 12,000 years. Today, many Wampanoag still live on ancestral lands
>   including what is now Cape Cod, Noepe (Martha's Vineyard), and Capoag
>   (Nantucket).

![A Mush8n](mush8n.png){:width="600" height="800}_A Mush8n_

In another location in the gardens is a
[wetu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetu),
a domed house made of branches and bark.

A building on the grounds contains a working carousel that was installed
in 1972. It has been painstakingly restored.

![Carousel](carousel.png){:width="800" height="600}_Carousel_

Another building contained a big exhibit about growing and harvesting
cranberries. I am now fully educated about cranberries.

The grounds contain a sundial with extensive information,
including the
[equation of time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equation_of_time),
for accurately determining local time.
(Unfortunately, the sun was behind clouds.)

![Sundial with Flume Fountain in the Background](sundial.png){:width="600" height="800}_Sundial with Flume Fountain in the Background_

I ate a delicious lunch of clam chowder and a chicken cranberry sandwich
with unsweetened iced tea at Lilly’s Café.

A replica of a Shaker round house contains the car museum, with beautifully
restored models of automobiles from the 1890s to the near present. My father
would have loved this museum.

![Replica of Shaker Round House](shaker_round_house.png){:width="800" height="600}_Replica of Shaker Round House_

I walked back to my campsite, arriving at 2:30 p.m.

## Afternoon Activities

At 3:15 p.m. I rode my bicycle into the part of Sandwich near the canal,
exploring the beach on Cape Cod Bay and the adjacent Sandwich Boardwalk. I rode
around the marina and continued on the Cape Cod Canal Trail to the Sagamore
Bridge before returning to the campground. I rode 14.12 miles according to my
watch, finishing at 5:15 p.m.

<iframe src="https://ridewithgps.com/embeds?type=trip&id=416267712&sampleGraph=true&privacyCode=zYS5JtLtxxa7x71Sjg3ujr7wo4jiSa9U" style="width: 1px; min-width: 100%; height: 700px; border: none;" scrolling="no"></iframe>

The weather in the afternoon was mostly sunny, 62 °F. Because I had eaten an
enormous lunch at Heritage Museum & Gardens, my dinner was cookies, crackers,
and string cheese.

I put on a stocking hat and walked another mile after dinner to get warm. The
owl I had heard Sunday night began hooting again at dusk. I climbed into my
sleeping bag at 7:20 p.m. Because the sky was clear, with a first quarter moon
low in the east, I pulled back the rain fly of my tent to allow moonlight to
bathe me in my sleep. I slept 7:30–9:00 p.m., listened to an audiobook for a
while, and fell back asleep for the rest of the night.
