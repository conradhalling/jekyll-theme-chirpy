Thursday, September 24, 2026, was the last of five days of camping at
[Shawme-Crowell State Forest](https://www.mass.gov/locations/shawme-crowell-state-forest)
in Sandwich, Massachusetts, on Cape Cod.

For the previous day of this five-day camping trip, see
[Cape Cod 2026, Day Four]( {% link _posts/2026-09-23-cape-cod-2026-day-four.md %} ).

## Activities

After falling asleep very early at 6:30 p.m. Wednesday night, I woke up for
two hours at 11:30 p.m. I went back to sleep from 1:30–6:00 a.m. for a total of
9.5 hours of sleep. I was still very tired from my 34-mile bicycle ride on
Tuesday.

Although the temperature was about 55 °F, I was a little chilly in my sleeping
bag, which is rated for 32 °F. (I know better than to believe this.) I have a
good inflatable sleeping pad that keeps me insulated from the ground, and I use
a silk blend sleeping bag liner for extra warmth. I put on light hiking socks to
keep my feet warm and a Fred Lebow Half Marathon stocking cap to keep my head
warm. These kept me warm all night. In colder temperatures, I would add long
polypro or wool underwear.

It was windy overnight, and because of an approaching nor'easter, I
decided to leave a day early. I had most of my things packed by 8:15 a.m. I
discovered that ants had gotten into my chocolate chip cookies (only three were
left), so I threw the cookies into the bushes for the ants to finish.

Since I was not in a hurry to leave, I did some walking and bicycling. At 8:35
a.m., I began walking on the hiking trails northwest of the campground.

I returned to my campsite at 10:35 a.m., having walked 4.46 miles in two hours
in sunny weather, 59 °F, with gusty winds.

I rode my bicycle on all of the paved campground roads. This was high intensity
interval training because the roads have some very steep (but short) hills. My
watch said I rode 4.61 miles.

Exercise finished, I resumed packing. I discovered that, as long as I removed
the pedals and turned the handlebars, I could put my Trek bike in a Honda Civic
hatchback without having to remove the front wheel. This encourages me to make
more day trips in a car, carrying my bike to begin a ride far from my home.

## The Drive Home

I left Shawme-Crowell State Forest at 12:30 p.m. Checkout time is
at 11:00 a.m., but I had paid for the site for one more day, so I didn't have
to worry about vacating the site on schedule.

Sometime in the future I want to camp, hike, and bicycle in
[Myles Standish State Forest](https://www.mass.gov/locations/myles-standish-state-forest),
which is only 24–26 miles from Shawme-Crowell State Forest by bicycle. Since I
was nearby, I drove to Myles Standish State Forest via Route 6A (Sandwich Rd.),
the Bourne Bridge, State Route 25, I-495, State Route 58, Tremont St., Cranberry
Rd., and Lower College Pond Rd., ending up near Three Cornered Lake after a
45-minute drive.

<iframe src="https://ridewithgps.com/embeds?type=trip&id=415982142&sampleGraph=true&privacyCode=kwzKzdr9yXUups8FaCW7Sk9KDRrg9Dmc" style="width: 1px; min-width: 100%; height: 700px; border: none;" scrolling="no"></iframe>

This route would not be suitable for bicycling. If I ride down
to Myles Standish from home, I would likely stay in the campground near Charge
Pond.

It was early cranberry harvest season; this area of Massachusetts is full of
cranberry fields, including an occasional flooded field where harvesting was
taking place.

I drove home from Myles Standish State Forest, leaving at 2:00 p.m. The route I
took, Tremont St. and Federal Furnace Rd. into Plymouth, and State Route 3A
heading north from Plymouth, is bikeable; but I would be unlikely to ride that
route.

I encountered very heavy stop and go traffic on State Route 3 approach Boston
and on I-93 through Boston. After putting gas in the car, I arrived home at
about 4:00 p.m.
