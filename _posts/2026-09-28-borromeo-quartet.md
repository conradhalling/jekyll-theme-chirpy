As part of my new life as a student of the cello, I am going to chamber music
concerts. Last night I heard the
[Borromeo String Quartet](https://necmusic.edu/events/faculty-recital-borromeo-string-quartet-3/)
play a faculty recital of three string quartets by
[Ludwig van Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven).

## Borromeo String Quartet

The
[Borromeo String Quartet](https://www.borromeoquartet.org/)
is the faculty String-Quartet-in-Residence at the
[New England Conservatory](https://necmusic.edu/) in Boston, Massachusetts.

The musicians are:

[Nicholas Kitchen, violin](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/nicholas-kitchen/)\
[Kristopher Tong, violin](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/kristopher-tong/)\
[Melissa Reardon, viola](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/melissa-reardon/)\
[Yeesun Kim, cello](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/yeesun-kim/)

## Program

[Beethoven: String Quartet No. 1 in F Major, op. 18 no. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_Quartet_No._1_(Beethoven))\
[Beethoven: String Quartet No. 9 in C Major, op. 59 no. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_Quartet_No._9_(Beethoven))

intermission

[Beethoven: String Quartet No. 12 in E-flat Major, op. 127](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_Quartet_No._12_(Beethoven))

## My Preparation

I prepared for this concert by listening to the appropriate individual
lectures from
<cite>[The String Quartets of Beethoven](https://robertgreenbergmusic.com/download/beethoven-string-quartets/)</cite>
by
[Robert Greenberg](https://robertgreenbergmusic.com/)
and
[The Great Courses](https://www.thegreatcourses.com).

I also downloaded the score for <cite>String Quartet No. 1</cite> and tried to
follow along while listening to a recording by the Artemis Quartet, but I
frequently got lost because the tempo was faster than I was capable of
following.

## Concert Notes

This concert was held at NEC's jewel of a music venue,
[Jordan Hall](https://necmusic.edu/on-campus/concert-halls-and-venue-rentals/#jordan-hall).
I arrived 35 minutes before
the start and waited in line with the other early birds until the doors were
opened. I was able to get an excellent seat in the middle of the third row. The
hall was about a third full.

The Borromeo Quartet strode onto the stage promptly at 7:30 p.m. They played
all three quartets beautifully and expertly.

I love the second movement of <cite>String Quartet No. 1</cite>, which has a
time signature of 9/8 (although I wouldn't have known that if I hadn't read the
score). The third movement (scherzo) is witty and would be fun to play.

The second movement of <cite>String Quartet No. 9</cite> is also very
interesting to me as a beginner cellist because the cello part involves a lot of
pizzicato, sometimes almost in the manner of an acoustic jazz bass.

<cite>String Quartet No. 12</cite>, the first of Beethoven's late quartets,
sounds modern and experimental, with many dissonances and unusual sounds.

During the concert, I focused my attention on
[Yeesun Kim](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/yeesun-kim/),
the cellist. She played flawlessly, displaying the ability to immerse herself
completely in the music.

According to the notes on NEC's website, "Yeesun Kim plays a Peregrino Zanetto
cello, circa 1576, one of the oldest in the world." This means her cello is
450 years old! It is a beautiful instrument.

## Other Notes

Robert Greenberg's course about Beethoven's string quartets, mentioned above, is
well worth the investment. I obtained the audio version of his course more than
ten years ago through my subscription with
[Audible](https://www.audible.com)
for about $13.

Emily E. Hogstad at
[Interlude](https://interlude.hk/)
published an article,
[Ranking the 16 Beethoven String Quartets: Which Is Best?](https://interlude.hk/ranking-the-16-beethoven-string-quartets-which-is-best/),
on September 21, 2026, in which she uses the metric of popularity on YouTube.

My mother, who was a cellist, used to play string quartets with friends in
the living room of our house in Portland, Oregon, when I was a child. I
expect they played string quartets by Beethoven, but I don't know, and my
mother died in 2022, so I can't ask her.
