Yesterday, October 2, 2026, I had my fourteenth cello lesson with
[Ana Ospina-Edwards](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/faculty/ospina)
at
[New School of Music](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org).

## Practice Session Notes

I keep a detailed practice journal, and I'm getting better at making a plan
before each practice session. I practice on average about sixty minutes per day,
but I feel like I need ninety minutes per day or more to cover all the material
completely.

My last lesson was two weeks ago because I went to Cape Cod for five days. I
practiced 6 hours 0 minutes before this lesson. I practiced scales with drones.
I worked on trying to get more control of my bow. One day I recorded myself
playing the D major scale, and I played along with it as a round.

From <cite>Suzuki Cello School Volume 1</cite>, I practiced "Allegro,"
"Perpetual Motion" in D major and G major, with variations, "Long, Long Ago,"
"Allegretto," and "Andantino." I played some of these with a backing track
at 60% or 70%; I was unable to play any of these at performance tempo.

From <cite>Blue Book of Tunes</cite>, I practiced "It's Raining" the morning
of my lesson.

## Lesson Notes

My bowing is very awkward, and I don't play smoothly. Ana had me practice
"painting" with my right hand to mimic how I should be drawing the bow. I need
to practice long bow strokes.

I asked about exercises for slurs, and it turns out Ana had assigned me an
exercise in <cite>Blue Book of Tunes</cite> but I had neglected it:  "Scales
with Slurs" on p. 38. Ana had me practice slurs while doing scales. Ana said
I should vary scales by playing triplets and other rhythms. Ana recommends doing
scales with a metronome and a drone, which I'm already doing. Ana also
recommends that I warm up by doing some of the earlier Suzuki pieces such as
"French Folk Song."

Ana observed that I'm pulling my left elbow in when playing on the A string, and
this is causing me to shift my fingers and play out of tune; I need to correct
this habit.

We played the duet "It's Raining" from <cite>Blue Book of Tunes</cite>. From the
same book, I played "Ode to Joy," which contains slurs. I played
"Perpetual Motion" from <cite>Suzuki Cello School Volume 1</cite>; I need to
improve my staccato.

From <cite>Suzuki Cello School Volume 1</cite>, Ana introduced me to "Rigadoon,"
which I have listened to but not played yet. That's my new Suzuki piece for the
next week.

I asked Ana about upcoming performances. She has joined the
[Boston Chamber Symphony](https://www.bostonchambersymphony.org/), and their
first performance this season is on Saturday, October 17, at First Church in
Cambridge; I walk by this church on my way to my lessons. Ana said her student
recital is coming up probably in November.
