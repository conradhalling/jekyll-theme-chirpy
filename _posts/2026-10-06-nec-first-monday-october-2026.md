Last night I went to
[New England Conservatory](https://necmusic.edu/)'s
[First Monday](https://necmusic.edu/events/first-monday-at-jordan-hall-reich-poulenc-and-beethoven/)
concert at Jordan Hall in Boston, Massachusetts. Various NEC faculty, students,
and alumni performed. The musicians were outstanding and the music was very
interesting.

## NEC's First Monday Series

From NEC's website:

> For more than 40 years, First Monday at Jordan Hall has been a cornerstone of
> New England Conservatory’s musical community, bringing together acclaimed
> faculty, distinguished alumni, exceptional students, and guest artists for
> unforgettable chamber music performances.

> Founded by renowned cellist
> [Laurence Lesser](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/laurence-lesser/)

> [Nicholas Cords](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/nicholas-cords/),

> and pianist
> [HaeSun Paik](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/haesun-paik/)

> discovery that define NEC.

## Introduction and “Meet the Artist” Interview

Before the concert,
[Soovin Kim](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/soovin-kim/)
gave a brief introduction and interviewed pianist and NEC alumna
[Zitong Wang](https://www.zitong-wang.com/)
about music competitions and whether the winners can still become famous and in
demand. Zitong Wang said she had performed at least sixty times in the past
year, and she had recently returned from a series of concerts in Japan.

## Steve Reich

The first piece on the program was
[Steve Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Reich):
<cite>Music for Pieces of
Wood</cite> (1973), played on, yes, _pieces of wood_ by:

[Robert Schulz](https://northcountrychamberplayers.org/project/schultz-robert-percussion/)\
[Yilin Chen](https://necmusic.edu/events/recital-yilin-chen-26-mm-contemporary-musical-arts/)\
[Avi Randall](https://www.passim.org/artists/avi-randall/)\
Bug Jaffe\
Elfie Shi

The pieces of wood are
hollow wooden cylinders tapped with solid wood cylinders (I suppose there are
technical names for these, but I don't know what they are). The piece starts out
simply but quickly grows in complexity with polyrhythms that I couldn't count in
my head. There are several recordings of this piece available as well as YouTube
videos. Steve Reich celebrated his 90th birthday on October 3, 2026.

## Francis Poulenc

[Dang Thai Son](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/thai-son-dang/)
and [Zitong Wang](https://www.zitong-wang.com/) (NEC Concert Artist)
played three pieces by
[Francis Poulenc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Poulenc)
for two pianos:

Francis Poulenc: <cite>Sonata for Piano Four-Hands, FP8</cite>\
Francis Poulenc: <cite>Élégie for Two Pianos, FP175</cite>\
Francis Poulenc: <cite>Capriccio d'après "le bal masqué", FP175</cite>

I enjoyed these pieces, which are modern but also melodic.

## Ludwig van Beethoven

After the intermission, eleven musicians played
[Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven):
<cite>Symphony No. 2 in D Major, op. 36</cite>,
arr. for chamber ensemble by
[Jopfen Music](https://jopfenmusic.com).

The musicians were:

[Soovin Kim](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/soovin-kim/), first violin\
[Sophie Wang](https://necmusic.edu/about/news/bso-violinist-sophie-wang-18-mm-20-gd-revisits-the-audition-process/), second violin\
[Cara Pogossian](https://carapogossian.com/), viola\
[Blaise Déjardin](https://www.blaisedejardin.com/), cello\
[Donald Palma](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/donald-palma/), double bass\
[Cynthia Meyers](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/cindy-meyers/), flute\
[John Ferrillo](https://necmusic.edu/faculty/john-ferrillo/), oboe\
[Nicholas Brown](https://www.nbclar.com/), clarinet\
[Richard Svoboda](http://richardsvoboda.com/), bassoon\
[Austin Ruff](https://www.bso.org/profiles/austin-ruff), French horn\
[Tim Genis](https://www.bso.org/profiles/timothy-genis), timpani

As mentioned by Soovin Kim in his introduction before the concert, this
transcription for chamber orchestra makes each instrumental voice stand out
clearly rather than blending in with the other instruments. Beethoven's
Second Symphony works particularly well for this treatment since it is not
complex yet is still Beethoven.
