Yesterday, October 9, 2026, I had my fifteenth cello lesson with
[Ana Ospina-Edwards](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/faculty/ospina)
at
[New School of Music](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org).

## Practice Session Notes

I practiced for 8 hours 30 minutes last week. I made a plan before each practice
session, I stayed focused on my goals for each session, and I took notes. On
Tuesday I practiced three times for a total of 2 hours 5 minutes. This
was productive because I reviewed old pieces and covered all of material I was
assigned, but I was mentally fatigued on Wednesday and Thursday, when I
practiced much less. I do not yet have the mental stamina for practicing much
more than an hour a day without losing enthusiasm.

In <cite>Blue Book of Tunes</cite>, I practiced "Scales with Slurs" for the D
major, G major, C major, and A minor scales, always using a metronome and
sometimes using a drone. After a week, the slurs began to feel less awkward
 under my fingers. I increased the tempo for all four scales from 60 to 80 bpm.
I practiced "Ode to Joy" with special focus on the slurs, and I worked on
memorization and increasing my tempo over the week from 60 bpm to 70, 80, 90,
and 100 bpm. I practiced the melody and harmony parts of "It's Raining," and I
recorded the harmony and played it back while I played the melody. I increased
my tempo to 80, 90, and 100 bpm, but the audio example is played at 120 bpm.

In <cite>Suzuki Cello School Volume 1</cite>, I practiced "Rigadoon," working on
the preparatory exercises first before playing the piece. I often forgot to play
C natural, automatically playing a C sharp after so many weeks of playing pieces
in D major. I could play "Rigadoon" at 50% and 60% of performance tempo when I
played with backing tracks. I continued working on "Andantino," reaching 70% of
performance tempo when playing with backing tracks. Looking through my notes, I
see that I practiced "Rigadoon" and "Andantino" very little compared to the
pieces in <cite>Blue Book of Tunes</cite>. To me, that suggests I'm trying to
cover too much material each week.

During my practice sessions, I tried to reduce tension by not squeezing the neck
as much and keeping my bow light on the strings. But when I'm not thinking about
this, I revert quickly to bad habits.

## Lesson Notes

At the start of my lesson, Ana always asks me how I'd like to start. This week,
I actually made a plan before my lesson, and I asked to start by playing scales
with slurs, using this to warm up my fingers. Ana wants me to play scales faster
(I play very slowly). I mentioned that I practice with a metronome to increase
my speed, but without the metronome I still play slowly. Ana says I need to
develop an internal metronome (that ticks faster).

From <cite>Blue Book of Tunes</cite>, we played "Ode to Joy" as a duet several
times. Ana wants me to play it faster, so I tried, but my internal metronome and
my fingers wouldn't let me go very fast. Ana says I should play "Ode to Joy" at
an upcoming student recital. I have always liked this tune from Beethoven's
<cite>Ninth Symphony</cite>.

From <cite>Suzuki Cello School Volume 1</cite>, we played "Rigadoon," and Ana
gave me suggestions for playing it more nicely. I kept making mistakes, and I
haven't memorized the entire piece. Looking back at my practice notes, I see I
didn't practice "Rigadoon" very much in the past week.

Ana introduced me to second position. She gave me a digital scan of Rick
Mooney's <cite>Position Pieces for Cello</cite>, and she demonstrated  "Target
Practice" on p. 3 before having me play it a couple of times. In this exercise,
I move between the following notes: open D on string II, D harmonic using my
ring finger on string II, D in first position using my fourth finger on string
I, and D in second position using my second finger on string I.

From <cite>Position Pieces for Cello</cite>, Ana also assigned me "Fanfare,"
but we didn't have time to play it. Ana marked where I'm to play in 2nd position
and where in 1st position.

I have ordered a paper copy of <cite>Position Pieces for Cello</cite> from
[Johnson String](https://www.johnsonstring.com); this book is not available
digitally from the distributer, [Alfred Music](https://www.alfred.com).
