Last night, October 17, 2026, I attended a concert of the
[Boston Symphony Orchestra](https://www.bso.org/), Andris Nelsons conducting,
at Symphony Hall in Boston, Massachusetts. I am writing this review to jog my
memories in future years; this is not intended to be a serious and scholarly
review.

The program was:

-   Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky: <cite>Polonaise from Eugene Onegin, Opus 24</cite>
-   Carlos Simon: <cite>Double Concerto for violin, cello, and orchestra</cite>
-   Pytor Ilyich Tchaikovsky: <cite>The Nutcracker, Opus 71, Act II</cite>

I was seated on the main floor towards the front and left, in seat I22. This
was close to the stage, giving me an excellent
view of Hilary Hahn and Seth Parker Woods during the double concerto.

Carlos Simon's <cite>Double Concerto</cite> was "co-commissioned by the
Boston Symphony Orchestra, Andris Nelsons, Music Director, through the generous
support of the New Works Fund established by the Massachusetts Cultural
Council, a state agency." I think it's good for governments
to support the arts.

Tchaikovsky's <cite>Polonaise</cite> was a light, insubstantial warm-up for
the orchestra and audience. I have not heard Tchaikovsky's opera
<cite>[Eugene Onegin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Onegin_(opera))</cite>,
so I had no context for this short piece.

[Carlos Simon](https://www.carlossimonmusic.com/)'s
<cite>Double Concerto</cite> with
[Hilary Hahn](https://hilaryhahntours.com/) and
[Seth Parker Woods](https://sethparkerwoods.com/)
was my reason for attending this concert. My wife and I attended a
performance of the [St. Louis Symphony Orchestra](https://slso.org/) in the
late 1990s when a young Hilary Hahn played
[Beethoven's <cite>Violin Concerto</cite>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin_Concerto_(Beethoven)),
a concert I remember well. It was a treat to see and hear Hilary Hahn again
after so many years. As a new student of the cello, I was also eager
to watch Seth Parker Woods play. Carlos Simon came on stage and gave a short
spoken introduction to the piece.

I enjoyed the <cite>Double Concerto</cite> very much as a piece of new music
that is not too dissonant
([Elliot Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Carter)
comes to mind as an example of dissonant music I do not understand). Hilary Hahn
and Seth Parker Woods coordinated very well together, and they clearly
understood each other and were enjoying playing the concerto. They both have
wonderful stage presence. My only negative comment about the performance would
be that sometimes the orchestra drowned out the soloists. I would like
to hear this concerto again, but I have schedule conflicts for the other two
performances by the BSO.

The audience enjoyed the performance and responded with a standing ovation,
calling the soloists, composer, and conductor back three times for bows.

After the intermission, the orchestra played Act II of Tchaikovsky's
<cite>Nutcracker</cite>, an odd choice a little early for the Christmas
season. Andris Nelsons will conduct all six of Tchaikovsky's symphonies in
the spring, so perhaps this was intended as a preview. Whatever, I loved this
performance; I think it's the first time I've heard the music, so familiar,
performed live. Of all the wonderful tunes in this piece, my favorite is
"Coffee (Arabian Dance)" with its unusual harmonies. The quality of playing
by the orchestra was outstanding.

The audience loved the <cite>Nutcracker</cite> (big surprise!) and responded
with a standing ovation and loud cheers. I find that young music students are
vocal in their appreciation of fine playing, and I support this attitude.

On my way home on the Green Line E train, about a hundred festive young women
boarded at North Station after attending a
[concert by Olivia Rodrigo](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/16/arts/olivia-rodrigo-joy-fury-empowerment-td-garden/).
