Yesterday, October 6, 2026, I had my sixteenth cello lesson with
[Ana Ospina-Edwards](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/faculty/ospina)
at
[New School of Music](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org).

## Practice Session Notes

I practiced for 9 hours 15 minutes last week, the most ever for me (this is
perhaps two days' practice for a *serious* music student). I made a plan before
each practice session, and I took notes. But I felt I had too much to work on
during the week:

-	<cite>Position Pieces for Cello</cite>
	-	"Target Practice," p. 3
	-	"Fanfare," p. 5
-	<cite>Suzuki Cello School Volume 1</cite>
	-	"Rigadoon," p. 18
	-	Tonalization and C Major Scale (Two Octaves), p. 19
	-	"Etude," p. 19
-	<cite>Blue Book of Tunes</cite>
	-	"Ode to Joy," pp. 34–35
	-	"Bow & Fiddle" ("Drunken Sailor"), p. 36
	-	"Scales with Slurs", p. 38

I made progress on all of these pieces, but I didn't feel like I *finished*
any one of them (where for me as a beginner *finished* means playing through
at 70–80% performance tempo with few or no errors).

During my practice sessions, I continue to struggle with tension.

## Lesson Notes

We started with "Scales with Slurs" in D major, G major, and two-octave C major.
I need to use the entire bow when playing scales. I need to bring my left elbow
forward for the G and C strings.

I played "Rigadoon," and Ana helped me improve bowing measures 9 and 13 more
smoothly by using just my lower arm, hinging at the elbow. These measures were
awkward for me because I was using too much upper arm.

I played "Target Practice," but I was inconsistent in establishing second
position. I am to work on this 5–10 minutes at the beginning of each practice
session.

I played "Etude," and Ana saw that I was awkward at playing some parts. She had
me pluck this piece, and she demonstrated variations that I can use while
practicing. I don't know why I didn't think of plucking the notes; I did this
while learning "Perpetual Motion."

This was another excellent lesson that left me eager to go home and resume
practicing.
