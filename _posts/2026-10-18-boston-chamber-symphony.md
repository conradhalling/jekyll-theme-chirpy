My cello teacher,
[Ana Ospina-Edwards](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/faculty/ospina),
recently joined the
[Boston Chamber Symphony](https://www.bostonchambersymphony.org/),
and she alerted me to the symphony's first concert, "Masterworks by Mozart,
Beethoven, and Brahms," presented last night (October 17, 2026) at First Church
in Cambridge, Massachusetts. The program was:

Mozart: <cite>Overture to Così fan tutte</cite>\
Beethoven: <cite>Symphony No. 8</cite>\
Brahms: <cite>Violin Concerto</cite>, Joshua Brown, soloist

[First Church in Cambridge](https://www.firstchurchcambridge.org/),
located adjacent to Cambridge Common, was an easy thirty-minute walk from my
house. (I walk by this church every week on my way to my cello lesson at
[New School of Music](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/).)
I found a seat in the third row of pews with a good view of the conductor, the
soloist, and the cello section.

[Mozart's <cite>Overture to Così fan tutte</cite>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cos%C3%AC_fan_tutte)
was a light introduction to the evening, a nice warm-up for the musicians and
listeners.

For me, the highlight of the evening was
[Beethoven's <cite>Symphony No. 8 in F major, Op. 93</cite>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._8_(Beethoven)),
which is a compact but brilliantly composed symphony containing many musical
jokes. I find the second movement, which ticks along like a metronome or clock,
especially fun.

The second half of the concert was devoted to
[Brahms's <cite>Violin Concerto in D major, Op. 77</cite>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin_Concerto_(Brahms)).
I am usually indifferent to Brahms, but I had never heard his violin concerto
before, and I greatly enjoyed it.
[Joshua Brown](https://www.joshuabrownviolinist.com/),
the violin soloist, was outstanding, playing with little effort a piece that was
originally considered unplayable. Brown received a standing ovation, and he
returned to play a short virtuoso piece that consisted seemingly entirely of
double stops (I don't know what the piece was).

[Avlana Eisenberg](https://www.bostonchambersymphony.org/team), the music
director who is also a
[professor at the Boston College Law
School](https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/schools/law/academics-faculty/faculty-directory/avlana-eisenberg.html),
did a wonderful job of conducting, with gestures that were easy to interpret
even by myself, a musical novice.

The orchestra itself is filled with young, skilled musicians who play
beautifully. I look forward to attending the remaining two concerts of the
2026/2027 season.

The person I was sitting next to introduced himself during the intermission; he
is Larry Green, a member of the board of directors of the Boston Chamber
Symphony. We had a nice conversation about chamber music in Boston and my
efforts to learn cello; Larry is a lawyer who plays piano.

There was a reception for the musicians after the performance, and Eli,
another one of Ana Ospina-Edwards's cello students, and I were able to chat with
her.
