Last night I went to a concert titled "Portraits of an American Patchwork" by
[Rasa String Quartet](https://www.rasastringquartet.com)
at the
[Museum of Modern Renaissance](https://modren.us/)
here in Somerville, Massachusetts. This is the description of the program
from Rasa String Quartet's website:

>   In celebration of America’s 250th birthday, Rasa String Quartet presents a
>   musical patchwork quilt, weaving together the cultural traditions that have
>   shaped the nation’s identity. Spanning jazz, bluegrass, Latin pop,
>   Indigenous traditions, global folk music, and classical favorites, the
>   program captures the vibrant creative spirit of American music. From the
>   soulful lyricism of William Grant Still and the Latin groove of Bad Bunny to
>   the infectious swing of Billy Strayhorn and the driving energy of Jerod
>   Impichchaachaaha’ Tate, An American Patchwork brings America’s multitude of
>   rich soundscapes all together on one stage.

The Rasa String Quartet is:

[Emma Powell](https://www.rasastringquartet.com/emma-powell), viola\
[Maura Shawn Scanlin](https://www.maurashawnscanlin.com/), violin\
[Kiyoshi Hayashi](https://kiyoshihayashi.com/), violin\
[Mina Kim](https://minakimcello.com), cello

The group opened the set with
the jazz standard
["Take the 'A' Train"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_the_%22A%22_Train)
by
[Billy Strayhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Strayhorn).

After playing a piece I didn't catch the name of (or recognize), the
quartet played an arrangement of
["Ashokan Farewell"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashokan_Farewell)
by
[Jay Ungar](https://www.jayandmolly.com/).

Later the group played "Pisachi" by
[Jerod Impichchaachaaha’ Tate](https://jerodtate.com/).

The quartet continued with a Korean lullaby called "Spring in My Hometown" by
Hong Nan-Pa; many recordings are available on music streaming services and on
YouTube.

The group followed with the three movements of
["Lyric Quartette"](https://www.editionsilvertrust.com/still-lyric-qt.htm)
by the American composer
[William Grant Still](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Grant_Still).

And finally, they finished with an arrangement of
["La Mudanza"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Mudanza)
by
[Bad Bunny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Bunny).

Many members of the Boston-area music community were in attendance, and the
audience responded with a standing ovation at the end of the concert.

This was my first visit to the Museum of Modern Renaissance, a building with a
beautiful and quirky interior.

![Museum of Modern Renaissance](museum_of_modern_renaissance.png){:width="800" height="600"}_Museum of Modern Renaissance_

Every concert I attend expands my education and knowledge of music. A year ago,
if I had heard "Take the 'A' Train," I couldn't have named the
tune. I became familiar with it last winter while I was teaching myself guitar.

Only two days ago I learned about William Grant Still from my brother, a
musician who is a member of the Hillsboro Symphony in Oregon, which is playing
several pieces by Still in their next concert.
