## Introduction

Yesterday afternoon, November 13, 2026, I went to <cite>A Golden Wire:
Timaeus</cite> at the [Boston Athanaeum](https://bostonathenaeum.org/), a
private library on Beacon Hill. My wife and I are members of the Athenaeum,
which enabled me to reserve my ticket early; the performance sold out almost
immediately.

This concert was part of the Boston Symphony Orchestra's two-week Creation
Festival, which included performances of
[Haydn's <cite>The Creation</cite>]( {% link _posts/2026-11-06-haydns-creation-at-bso.md %}),
the commission of
[Golijov's <cite>Creation</cite>]( {% link _posts/2026-11-15-golijov-creation.md %}),
and other smaller events I wasn't able to attend. From the BSO's website:

>   The Creation festival, Golijov commission, and Humanities events are
>   generously supported by Catherine and Paul Buttenwieser. Additional generous
>   support for the Creation Festival from Ann Merrifield and Wayne Davis.

Quoting from the Boston Symphony Orchestra's web page for this individual event:

>   As part of the Boston Symphony Orchestra’s Creation Festival, A Golden Wire
>   offers a richly textured meditation on music, cosmology, and creation. The
>   intimate program draws on Plato’s <cite>Timæus</cite> and the ancient idea
>   of the “music of the spheres,” where the proportions governing the cosmos
>   mirror those of the musical scale.

>   Through English Elizabethan and Jacobean works alongside Italian
>   instrumental music, A Golden Wire illuminates a worldview in which harmony
>   shapes not only sound, but the structure of the universe itself—from the
>   movements of planets to the human body and voice. Blending performance with
>   intellectual discovery, this program invites listeners to hear creation as
>   the ancients imagined it: ordered, resonant, and profoundly musical.

## A Golden Wire

[A Golden Wire](https://www.agoldenwire.com/) is an early
music ensemble based in New York:

> A Golden Wire specializes in 17th century music of England and the British
> Isles, where the harp and viol were particularly prominent. The ensemble’s
> repertoire also spans further, from the German Renaissance, to the high
> Baroque in France and Italy.

The [ensemble](https://www.agoldenwire.com/about/the-ensemble) includes:

Parker Ramsay, harp\
Arnie Tanimoto, viola da gamba\
Thomas Fields, viola da gamba\
Lucine Musaelian, voice, viola da gamba

## Timaeus

[Timaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timaeus_(dialogue)) refers to a dialog
by Plato that discusses the creation of the earth, heaven, gods, humans, and
animals. This charming (but hopelessly wrong) creation story establishes a
numerical organization to the universe, to humans, and even to musical
creativity. From the program notes:

> The implications for the relationship between music and humans are profound,
> as in such a paradigm, musical artistry and creativity are not reflective of
> any human will, but of a scientific organizational theme which binds humanity
> together.

The lyrics to the songs in the program address creation, the position of humans
in the universe, the passing of time, eternity, and finally death.
