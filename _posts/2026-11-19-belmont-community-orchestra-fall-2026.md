## Introduction

One of my goals in learning to play the cello is to try playing with an
orchestral ensemble. A recent article in
[The Boston Music Intelligencer](https://classical-scene.com/),
[Playing With Others Again: A Guide for Boston’s Adult Amateurs](https://classical-scene.com/2026/10/15/adult-amateurs/),
provides some guidance in this quest.

## Belmont Community Orchestra

An ensemble I'm considering is the
[Belmont Community Orchestra](https://powersmusic.org/program/belmont-community-orchestra/)
at [Powers Music School](https://powersmusic.org/)
in Belmont, Massachusetts, about 5.3 miles from where I live.

## Qualifications

These are the qualifications from the website:

>   To help set everyone up for success, musicians should feel comfortable with
>   the basics of their instrument, including reading first position notes,
>   playing simple rhythms, and having confidence with foundational scales (such
>   as G major for violin and C major for viola and cello). Beyond these
>   essentials, the most important qualities are a willingness to try, openness
>   to risk-taking, and commitment to rehearsing and performing with others.

After nineteen cello lessons, I can read first position notes (and I'm currently
learning second position and extended first position). My instructor says my
sense of rhythm is good (I'm the son of a drummer), and I can play several
one-octave scales (G major, E minor, D major, A minor, and F major) and
the two-octave C scale.

## Marshunda Smith, Conductor

The orchestra is directed by
[Marshunda Smith](https://powersmusic.org/people/marshunda-smith/);
[Ms. Smith](https://www.marshunda.com/) is a conductor, teacher, and cellist.

## Fall 2026 Concert

The program for the concert was:

-   Norman Dello Joio’s Choreography: Three Dances for String Orchestra, by the
    Pulitzer Prize-winning American composer
-   Richard Meyers’ Incantations
-   Adoration by Florence Price, the first Black woman composer to have a
    symphony performed by a major American orchestra, heard here in a version
    for strings
-   Loretta Lazaris’s School-Time Suite, written so that every player in the
    room has a complete line of their own

In last night's performance, the orchestra had ten cellos, eight violins, six
violas, one piano, and one bass. I noticed that some of the cellos were playing
along with the bass, probably to balance the sound. I watched the cellists
carefully; some were using vibrato, most weren't (I haven't learned to vibrate
yet). Most of the notes were played in first position, which occasional
shifting. The bowings were not complicated.

The conductor, Marshunda Smith, introduced each piece, and she encouraged
applause between movements. Ms. Smith is very outgoing and approachable with a
great sense of humor and a strong sense of justice and equity. I think she's a
huge asset that makes the orchestra successful.

## Spring Session

Registration for the spring session starts December 1. I will consult first with
my cello instructor, Ana Ospina-Edwards, and if she says I'm ready, I will apply
to join the orchestra. The spring session comprises ten rehearsals on Monday
nights followed by a concert in mid-May.  To get from my house to Powers Music
School for each rehearsal requires riding a bus, the subway, and a bus. I expect
that I will take a Lyft to and from each rehearsal since taking a cello on the
public transportation is not very convenient.
