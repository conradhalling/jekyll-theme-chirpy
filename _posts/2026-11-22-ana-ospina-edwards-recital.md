## Introduction

Last night, November 21, 2026, Ana Ospina-Edwards presented her
Graduating Student Project, [Echos](https://longy.edu/calendar/gsp-ana-ospina-edwards/),
at Edward M. Pickman Hall,
[Longy School of Music](https://longy.edu), in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

Ana provided some additional information in an email to her students, friends,
colleagues, and mentors. (I am one of Ana's cello students.)

>   I have curated this concert as a reflection on the people and experiences
>   that have influenced and supported me throughout both my personal and
>   professional life over the past few years. I chose the title
>   <cite>Echoes</cite> because I have come to realize that experiences from
>   years ago, along with recent ones, resonate deeply within me and have played
>   an important role in shaping who I am today.

## Program

<cite>Präludium I from Fünf Stücke</cite>\
Dmitri Shastakovich (1906–1975)\
transcription by Patricia di Carli (b. 1963)

<cite>Amaroso, Valse Lente Op. 160</cite>\
August Nöick (1862–1928)

<cite>Romance Op. 69</cite>\
Gabriel Fauré (1845–1924)

<cite> Lied Ohne Worte Op. 109</cite>\
Felix Mendelssohn (1809–1847)

<cite>Ballade Op. 4</cite>\
Reinhold Glière (1875-1956)

<cite>Trio for Violin, Violoncello and Pianoforte</cite>\
Rebecca Clarke (1886–1979)\
1\. Moderato ma appassionato

<cite>Melody Op. 4</cite>\
Arthur Foote (1853–1937)

<cite>Cant dels Ocells</cite>\
Traditional Catalan Song, arranged by Pau Casals (1876–1973)

<cite>Tanti Anni Prima</cite>\
Astor Piazzola (1921-1992)

<cite>Libertango</cite>\
Astor Piazzola (1921–1992)

This was a splendid project, beautifully conceived by Anna and played by Ana and
her colleagues. Ana's project embraced a wide variety of music and styles with
interesting, challenging, and often fun pieces from the nineteenth and twentieth
centuries. The program included a visual
component of images and poetry projected on a screen over the stage, including
a poem written in 1951 by Ana's father.

## Ana Ospina-Edwards's Bio

This is Ana's bio, copied from Longy's website, with revisions and links
added by me:

>   Ana is a Boston-based cellist and educator from Colombia. She combines
>   performance, teaching, and arts administration, serving as cello faculty at
>   [Attacca School of Music](https://www.attaccaschoolofmusic.com/)
>   and [The New School of Music](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/),
>   and coordinating
>   [Longy’s Side-by-Side program](https://longy.edu/study/longy-side-by-side/).

>   Passionate about multicultural chamber music,
>   Ana promotes concerts featuring underrepresented composers and performs solo
>   works that highlight the cello’s versatility across diverse traditions. She
>   plays with Boston-area orchestras, including the
>   [Boston Chamber Symphony](https://www.bostonchambersymphony.org/) and
>   [Du Bois Orchestra](https://www.duboisorchestra.org/),
>   where she is Personnel Manager. She is also the cellist
>   of [Eric Hofbauer EHX](https://www.erichofbauer.com/ehx),
>   whose 2026 album blends rock, jazz, and
>   new wave with social and cultural themes centered on reconnection through
>   music.

>   An Equity Scholar at Longy pursuing a graduate diploma, Ana is
>   committed to using music to foster multicultural dialogue and strengthen
>   community connections through her multifaceted artistic journey.

>   Ana Ospina-Edwards is a student of
>   [Christine Lamprea](https://longy.edu/faculty/christine-lamprea/) (cello)
>   and
>   [Jean-Michel Fonteneau](https://longy.edu/faculty/jean-michel-fonteneau/)
>   (Baroque cello).
