[Peter Paetkau](https://www.peterpaetkau.com),
a violinist who also [teaches violin](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/faculty/paetkau)
at [New School of Music](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org) in Cambridge,
Massachusetts, presented a
[faculty concert](https://www.newschoolofmusic.org/events/free-faculty-concert-series-peter-paetkau-violin-1)
yesterday afternoon, November 22, 2026.

## Program

I copied this description of the program from the
[Upcoming](https://www.peterpaetkau.com/upcoming-1)
page of Peter's website:

>   Vignettes, a concert for violin & piano, is made up of pieces that each
>   explore a theme or image. From the songlike qualities of Romantics, to the
>   imagery of today’s composers, this program brings the audience inside the
>   thought behind the music. A starry night, the nuances of grief, a celtic
>   fiddle, a German hymn, and a rain song all feature as inspiration for the
>   pieces included on this program. Join us to explore the ways these ideas
>   have been transformed into sound.

[Reena Esmail](https://www.reenaesmail.com/) (India/USA) - <cite>Darshan</cite>\
[Mai Ohtani](https://lit.link/en/maiohtani) (Japan) - <cite>Face up</cite>\
[Angélica Negrón](https://www.angelicanegron.com/) (Puerto Rico) - <cite>A Través del Manto Luminoso</cite>\
[Johannes Brahms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Brahms) (Germany) - <cite>[Sonata for Violin and Piano in G Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin_Sonata_No._1_(Brahms))</cite>

Peter, a founding member of the chamber music collective
[Analogue by Choice](https://www.analogbychoice.com/),
also teaches how to listen to music, and he asked members of
the audience to draw on paper with color pencils during the first three pieces
and to play with modeling clay during the Brahms violin sonata. The idea was
for us to capture our thoughts, feelings, and creative impulses as they were
stimulated by the music.

The first three pieces, modern and short, were very interesting and different.
Reena Esmail's <cite>Darshan</cite> explores feelings of grief in a blend of
Indian and European music. Mai Ohtani is a Japanese violinist interested in
Celtic fiddle music, and <cite>Face Up</cite> had a definite Celtic influence.
Angélica Negrón's
<cite>[A Través del Manto Luminoso](https://www.inacircle-records.com/httpsangelica-negron)</cite>
was inspired by gazing at the starry night; this piece included computer music
played from Peter's laptop.

The final piece was a more traditional offering, Brahms's first published sonata
for violin and piano, where Esther Ning Yao played the piano part. For me,
this music had a nice blend of violin and piano, and Peter and Esther played
the piece beautifully.

This was a very creative and different program of music.
