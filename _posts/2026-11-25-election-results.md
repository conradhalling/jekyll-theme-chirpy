I'm a coastal liberal and progressive with views that don't align with the views
of most voters in the United States. By choice, I live in Somerville,
Massachusetts, a deep blue city in a deep blue state where my views are
mainstream. I believe government is a system empowered by the voters for helping
the residents of our country, not to hold them down.

I was surprised and gratified by size of the blue wave that rolled through the
United States on election day. The voters rejected the corrupt
administration of Donald Trump and the Republican office-holders who passively
looked the other way. Voters reached their limits with violent immigration
control; racial discrimination; deficit spending; restrictive abortion laws;
gerrymandering; the teaching of Christianity in public schools; reduced funding
for education, science, and the arts; decreased help to the poor; pandering to
Elon Musk and other billionaires; and opposition to diversity, equity, and
inclusion.

Astonishingly, Texas turned into a blue state. Governor Greg Abbott, who ran for
re-election, and Attorney General Ken Paxton, who ran for U.S. Senate, were two
of the leaders of what I think of as the Texas Taliban; both were soundly
rejected. Paxton is deeply corrupt, and Abbott supported unconstitutional
policies for teaching Christianity in the public schools and other radical
conservative positions that were bad for Texas and its residents.

Here in New England, Susan Collins, one of the last moderate Republicans, is
also out. She has frustrated and annoyed progressive voters for many years
because she said some things that made her sound almost progressive, but then
she voted with the conservatives. When the conservatives then did things that
damaged the country and the state of Maine, she expressed her "concern."

Astonishingly, the Democratic caucus will hold 56 of the 100 seats in the U.S.
Senate and at least 259 out of 435 seats in the House. These margins are not
sufficient to override vetoes by President Trump, but they are large enough for
Congress to investigate corruption and lawlessness in the Trump administration.
They will also stop approving Trump's nominations of incompetent and reckless
persons for federal judge positions.

What is perhaps even more important is that Democrats did well in state
elections, too, even in what were considered deeply conservative states. Because
of federalism under the Constitution, states retain a great deal of power.

I'm still waiting for voters in Missouri and Florida, what used to be swing
states, to figure out that they've been voting for the wrong people. Florida
just gets increasingly worse; now the voters reduced property taxes, and they
will discover that their cities and towns will no longer be able to afford to
fund basic services.

I have some words of caution. The Democratic caucus needs to demonstrate that it
can work effectively. The new Congress must assert its powers and rights
under the Constitution to oppose the Trump adminstration and the conservative
Supreme Court. Also, many politicians — conservative and liberal — are corrupt
and unethical. Some with betray their constituents, and we must be on our
guard.
